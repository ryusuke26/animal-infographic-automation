## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted scientific name: *Calotriton arnoldi* Carranza & Amat, 2005.
- English public name: Montseny Brook Newt, supported by Amphibian Species of the World and Barcelona Zoo.
- Japanese public display name: モンセニーイモリ. No established standard Japanese common name was confirmed in Japanese institutional or general-audience results; this is a transparent Japanese display rendering of the supported English and Catalan locality name. The caveat stays in evidence records and source/context copy, not on the poster.
- Editorial classification group: Amphibians.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Amphibia; Caudata; Salamandridae; Pleurodelinae; *Calotriton*.
- Broad native region: Montseny Massif, northeastern Catalonia, Spain / Europe.
- Taxonomy caveat: a 2024 peer-reviewed revision recognizes two subspecies, *C. a. arnoldi* and *C. a. laietanus*. The poster remains at species level and uses traits shared or explicitly described across the species rather than assigning the hero to a subspecies.

## Exactly three public claims

1. Habitat: it inhabits cold, highly oxygenated mountain streams in beech and holm-oak forest.
2. Visible identity: its body is flattened and brown, with yellow or silvery-gold markings and no dorsal crest.
3. Behavior/adaptation: claw-like toe tips help it grip the rocky streambed in strong current.

## Visual identity lock

- One dominant adult in a stable dorsal three-quarter pose, lying close to a submerged rock in a narrow shaded mountain stream.
- Long but compact salamandrid body, broad flattened head with small dark eyes, flattened torso, four short connected limbs, four fingers and five toes, and a laterally compressed tail about as long as head and body.
- Chocolate-brown dorsal surface with restrained yellow-gold flecks or side markings, cream underside glimpsed only at the edge, granular skin, and no dorsal or tail crest.
- Each visible limb must have one traceable shoulder or hip origin, a separate path, and a separate endpoint with negative space from its near/far counterpart. Natural far-side occlusion is allowed only when the rock and body clearly explain it.
- Habitat must read as a cold, clear, steep Montseny forest brook with cracked slate or granite, moss, a few beech or holm-oak leaves, and shallow moving water—not a pond, aquarium, tropical waterfall, dry forest floor, or open river.
- Nearest false silhouettes to avoid: generic fire salamander, crested newt, axolotl, gecko, skink, eel, Pyrenean Brook Newt caricature, and any bright orange-bellied or high-crested newt.

## Discovery lock

Lead with the quiet physical problem of living in current: water presses downhill, while a small flattened animal keeps contact with rock using tiny claw-like toe tips. Let the story move from cold forest stream to body profile to grip. Conservation remains a quiet footer.
