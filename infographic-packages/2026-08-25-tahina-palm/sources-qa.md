## Exactly three public claims

1. Habitat: it grows in seasonally flooded soil at the foot of a limestone outcrop in northwestern Madagascar.
2. Visible identity: its enormous fan-shaped leaves can reach about 5 m across.
3. Life history: it sends up one huge terminal branching inflorescence and dies after flowering and fruiting.

## Visual identity lock

- One dominant complete flowering adult palm rooted at the base of a dark grey limestone outcrop. It has one solitary thick trunk with a visibly swollen base and horizontal ring scars; never multiple equal trunks and never a cluster of separate palms.
- The crown is a dense near-spherical burst of very large costapalmate fan leaves, not feather-shaped coconut fronds. Each leaf has a broad pleated fan blade divided into many stiff segments, and several old brown dead leaves hang below the green crown.
- The flowering structure starts from the exact top center of the crown as one pale upright terminal axis. It rises several metres above the fan leaves and branches repeatedly into an airy pyramidal candelabra carrying many small yellowish flowers. It is not a second tree, pine, antler, coral, firework, or detached flower cloud.
- Habitat is low-elevation northwestern Madagascar at a limestone foot: rough dark karst, seasonally wet fertile soil, dry-season scrub and grasses, bright tropical sky. Do not turn it into a humid rainforest, beach coconut scene, manicured garden, or desert oasis.
- Closest false silhouettes to avoid: coconut palm or date palm with feather leaves; banana plant; ordinary ornamental fan palm with a clean trunk; multi-stem palmetto; *Corypha* lookalike without the swollen, heavily scarred solitary trunk and retained dead leaves; agave, cycad, or yucca rosette.
- Card 1 spot art: one small complete palm rooted where seasonally wet soil meets a limestone foot, with no fake map.
- Card 2 spot art: one pleated circular fan leaf seen from below with a human-free scale cue from the trunk/crown, not a detached decorative rosette.
- Card 3 spot art: one small complete flowering palm showing the terminal candelabra rising from the crown and a quiet fallen-fruit/withering continuation, not three lifecycle panels and not a floating branch.
