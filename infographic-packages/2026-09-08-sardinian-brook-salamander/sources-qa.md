### Candidate 1 — Sardinian Brook Salamander / *Euproctus platycephalus* — selected

- Global familiarity check: English exposure is concentrated in European amphibian, zoo-husbandry and conservation material; Japanese general-audience exposure is sparse and no stable Japanese common name was found. - Discovery doorway: in running water, the male holds the female with his jaws and curves his body so their tail bases overlap before spermatophore transfer; females later fix eggs beneath stones and in crevices.

No established standard Japanese common name was confirmed in Japanese institutional or general-audience results; the display name is a transparent short rendering of the English name and Italian `Tritone Sardo`. - Broad native region: Sardinia, Italy / Mediterranean Europe.
- Habitat: mountain streams, calm parts of rivers, pools, small lakes and some cave systems; adults often walk along the bottom among stones and rubble.
- Curiosity hook: a flattened stream newt uses its jaws, curved body and tail position to keep a mating pair aligned in running water.

- Natural pose: complete adult in low dorsal three-quarter side view, walking along a calm stony stream bottom. The near-side limbs, flat head, mottled back and laterally compressed tail must be clear. A far-side limb may be partly hidden by the body or stones; no limb is forced into view.

- Uncertainty: photographs and descriptions support macroscopic anatomy and age-linked color variation, but not the hero's sex or exact spur anatomy. Sex, spur count and gill remnants are not claimed or illustrated.
