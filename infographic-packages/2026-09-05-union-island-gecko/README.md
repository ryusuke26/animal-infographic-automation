## Locked scope

- Accepted name: *Gonatodes daudini* Powell & Henderson, 2005.
- English public name: Union Island Gecko; Grenadines Clawed Gecko is a supported alias.
- Japanese public name: ダウディンイロワケヤモリ, used by Japan's Ministry of the Environment.
- Global status: IUCN Red List Critically Endangered (CR) under B1ab(iii)+2ab(iii), assessed 16 February 2019, record `T194258A71748321`.
- Public claims: it lives in mature dry upland forest on Union Island; adult males have a red-orange iris and three pairs of white-centered black-and-red eyespots; individuals cling to the upper surfaces of rock crevices.
- Discovery doorway: a gecko only a few centimetres long carries a structured head pattern, one crown eyespot, and three body eyespots on each side while disappearing into a shaded rock crevice.

## Earlier Rescue Run history

- Japanese generation 1 passed the mechanical source gate but was rejected because the three dorsolateral pairs read as one central row and Card 3 contained extra limbs. Both generated copies were deleted at the user's request.
- Fresh Japanese generation 2 corrected the six paired body ocelli and four-limb Card 3 but simplified the head into an overly plain olive field.
- User-authorized fresh Japanese generation 3 added generic head texture but collapsed the required six dorsolateral body ocelli into unpaired single rows.
- A nominal head-only Image Gen edit of generation 2 changed 99.96599% of the canvas, so it was rejected as a global redraw rather than composited into accepted art.
- Later fresh generations still treated the head as generic mottling rather than the structured live-color pattern in the original species description. User review correctly rejected them.
- Web text and image research reopened the Visual Lock: the adult male head is olive-gray with scattered black scales, two bright olive-green lines running from the nostrils to above and behind the eyes, postocular bright bands converging on the nape, and a separate occipital white spot ringed black and pale red. The three paired dorsolateral body ocelli begin behind this head/occipital system.
- Search-image results were filtered by exact taxon identity. The retained comparison set comprises the two Frank Deschandol/iNaturalist views plus two Joel Sartore Photo Ark views of an identified Oakland Zoo animal; unrelated red-eyed lizards returned by image search were excluded.
