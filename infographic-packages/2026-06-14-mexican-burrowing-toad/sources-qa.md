## Locked Public Claims

1. *Rhinophrynus dorsalis* lives in Mesoamerican lowland savannas and
   seasonally dry forests, with a native range extending from extreme southern
   Texas and Mexico to northwestern Costa Rica.
2. It spends much of the dry season underground and digs backward using paired
   enlarged spade-like structures on the inner metatarsal area of its hind
   feet.
3. Heavy rains bring it above ground, and males call in temporary pools during
   the breeding season.

## Visual Identity Guidance

Show one adult Mexican Burrowing Toad in a safe three-quarter view at the edge
of a shallow temporary rain pool, with damp dark soil and low seasonally dry
forest or savanna vegetation. The animal must have one coherent rounded,
almost oval dark brown to nearly black body with loose, slightly folded skin;
one very small pointed triangular head; two tiny eyes; no visible external
eardrum; four short robust limbs; and normal frog feet. Show scattered yellow,
orange, or reddish-orange spots and blotches. A narrow pale orange dorsal
stripe is optional because it varies among individuals. A small inset may show
the animal digging backward into soil, with paired enlarged spade-like
structures on the inner metatarsal area of the hind feet, not behind the heels
and not exaggerated into claws.

Avoid a generic true toad with a broad head, large parotoid glands, warty dry
skin, or a visible tympanum. Avoid spadefoot-toad vertical pupils, horned-frog
mouth proportions, purple-frog tubular snout, turtle shell, mammal-like claws,
extra toes, extra limbs, duplicated heads, front-first digging, deep permanent
ponds, tropical rainforest canopy, fake maps, people, rescue imagery,
population counters, eggs, and tadpoles.
