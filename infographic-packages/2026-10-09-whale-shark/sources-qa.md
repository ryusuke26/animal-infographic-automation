## Compact visual identity brief

Individual/stage: large free-swimming Rhincodon typus, sex unspecified; no precise age, measured length, or known individual. Reference 1 IUCN Pedro Vieyra/Marine Conservation Society Seychelles frontal feeding photo: broad terminal mouth, intact lips, flattened head and tiny side eye. Reference 2 Erik Schlögl/Australian Museum Ningaloo 27 April 2009 three-quarter image: full-body taper, pectoral attachment, larger first/smaller second dorsal, vertical caudal geometry, cream spots and faint bars. Florida Museum diagnostic description settles five gill slits, three low flank ridges and tail-stage variation.

Five defining features: (1) broad flat head and terminal transverse mouth at front margin, not ventral mouth behind pointed snout (ref1 head); (2) slate-blue dorsal skin, cream spots plus pale crosswise/longitudinal lines, white belly (ref2 upper head/body); (3) five gill slits just behind head and ahead of pectoral attachment (Florida diagnostic anatomy); (4) two dorsal fins, first substantially larger, attached broad pectoral pair and coherent pelvic/anal sequence (ref2 silhouette/Florida description); (5) vertical two-lobed tail, moderately larger upper lobe and tapering peduncle; no extreme juvenile asymmetry (ref2/Florida).
Natural hero pose: slightly elevated lateral three-quarter cruising, modest mouth opening, coherent near pectoral and far-fin occlusion. Belly, sex organs, far gills, far pelvic/pectoral can hide. No forced fin display. Miniature upright feeding is a separate complete educational study; no hidden external structures or internal filter exposure, so specialized everted-aperture rule is not triggered.
False silhouettes: great white's pointed snout/ventral mouth; whale's blowhole/horizontal flukes; manta's wing-shaped body. Do not infer unreadable fine teeth or exact spots. Spot arrangement is illustrative, not a photo-identification record. Prey art enlarged for explanation; water column schematic, depth not calibrated.
