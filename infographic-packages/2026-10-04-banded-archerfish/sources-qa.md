## Compact visual identity brief

- Individual/stage: adult-sized or subadult T. jaculatrix, sex unspecified. Sources do not require sex-specific external marks; no hatchling/adult transition claim.
- Reference roles: references/aquamarine-lateral.jpg shows entire flank, head, markings and fin attachments; references/aquamarine-front.jpg shows laterally compressed profile and compact terminal mouth. Captive photographs support geometry only. Warmth/strokes: existing purple-frog benchmark, style only.
- Defining features: (1) deep oval/rhomboid laterally compressed body, pointed sloping forehead and rounded belly, lateral reference whole silhouette; (2) silver/olive body with dark wedge-shaped marks mostly descending from upper flank, lateral image back/side; pattern varies, do not impose one universal exact count; (3) large round eye and compact upward-directed mouth with projecting lower jaw, lateral head/front photograph, Fishes of Australia mouth description; (4) one continuous dorsal fin on posterior back opposite anal fin, pale pectoral attached behind gill cover, narrow peduncle and pale truncate-to-shallow-notched tail, lateral reference; no deeply forked tuna tail; (5) no whiskers, long beak, limbs or teeth protruding externally.
- Natural pose: stable near-horizontal lateral hero swimming just beneath water under a mangrove branch. Slight upward snout is allowed; far-side paired fins may be hidden by the compressed body. Trace visible fins to body. The jumping study remains a separate card, not a distorted main silhouette.
- False silhouettes: not striped angelfish with tall sails; not T. chatareus with alternating large/small flank spots; not elongated trout or tuna.
- Shooting geometry: external aperture is the compact mouth at the snout tip, opening forward/upward; one unpaired water jet leaves that aperture. No internal tissue exposed and no paired extruded structure; bilateral-count issue is not applicable to a single jet. Side-on camera keeps eye, head and gill cover intact. No invented cutaway, tongue hose or opening across the cheek.
