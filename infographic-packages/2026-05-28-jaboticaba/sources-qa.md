# Sources and QA

## Source Notes

- Kew Plants of the World Online treats *Plinia cauliflora* as an accepted species and gives the native range as eastern Bolivia and Brazil from Pernambuco to Paraná, growing primarily in the seasonally dry tropical biome.
- Kew's general-information page lists IUCN-linked conservation as `LC - least concern`; its Useful Plants and Fungi of Colombia data records `IUCN Red List Assessment (2021): LC`.
- Makino Botanical Garden records the Japanese name `ジャボチカバ`, alternate `キブドウ`, family `フトモモ科`, scientific name *Plinia cauliflora* (Mart.) Kausel, and flowers that open directly from the trunk before edible fruit develops.
- Britannica describes jaboticaba as *Plinia cauliflora*, a myrtle-family tree native to southeastern Brazil, with small four-petaled white flowers growing directly on branches and trunk and maroon-purple berries.

## Publication-Safe Decisions

- Use `ジャボチカバ` and `Jaboticaba` as the main public names.
- Use `IUCN Red List 2021: Least Concern (LC)` as the status footer.
- Avoid population numbers.
- Avoid threat language and any rescue/blame framing.
- Keep `Brazilian grape tree` out of the main poster to reduce the chance that the visual becomes a grape vine.

## Image QA

- Japanese Image Gen PNG: usable. It shows a mottled trunk, white flowers, dark purple berries attached directly to the bark, leaf sprigs, and a fruit cross-section. Japanese title and scientific name are legible enough, but text-safe assets remain authoritative.
- English Image Gen PNG: usable. It shows trunk-attached flowers and berries, oval leaves, and a fruit cross-section. It does not show a vine, cacao pods, fake map, or threat imagery.
- Text-safe SVG/PNG backups are required because generated image text can drift and because final social posting needs exact Japanese and scientific spelling.
