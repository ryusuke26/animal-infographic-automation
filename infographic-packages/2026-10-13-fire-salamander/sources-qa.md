## Compact visual identity brief
Adult sex unspecified, reference-supported spotted yellow-black phenotype; no exact subspecies or body-length claim. Larva is a separate younger stage, not adult recolouring. Some populations vary to stripes/orange and some birth land-ready young; no universal pattern/reproduction claim.
Biological reference roles: references/mme-adult.jpg = MME Judit Vörös full-body pattern, stout trunk, four-limb topology and tapering tail; references/mme-portrait.jpg = MME Bálint Halpern head/nape/eye and raised yellow gland immediately behind eye; references/jlu-larva.webp = JLU Gießen larval front view with brown mottling and lateral external gill fans. Existing generated_infographics/purple_frog_ja_2026-04-28.png inspected as style benchmark only.
Defining features:
1. Adult full-body reference: broad rounded head continuously attached to stout cylindrical trunk; four short limbs from shoulder/hip positions; one round tapering tail, no adult crest or fin.
2. Adult photo zones: irregular yellow patches on black head, trunk, limbs and tail; no regular circular two-row Ambystoma spots. Pattern variability is illustrative, not an individual identification record.
3. Portrait: prominent dark eyes and oblong raised yellow parotid directly behind each eye on nape; never cheek pouch, horns or forehead gland.
4. Forefeet four short digits and hindfeet five where readable, supported by Kyiv morphology; no claws/scales. Natural body/rock occlusion accepted; do not force hidden limbs/toes visible.
5. Larval front reference: grey-brown speckled head/body and bilateral attached gill fans at lateral rear head; pale short forelimbs, no adult yellow-black pattern or adult external gills.
Natural pose: stable dorsal three-quarter adult walking over mossy rock; far limbs may partially hide behind trunk/rock. Larva shown in front view matching acquired photo, not a speculative lateral whole-body mechanism. Head-and-neck inset stays attached/contextual. No exposed aperture/inside-out anatomy is needed.
False silhouettes: black Alpine Salamander lacks this widespread yellow pattern; Ambystoma maculatum typically orderly round paired spots; lizard has scales/claws. No transformation into those taxa.
