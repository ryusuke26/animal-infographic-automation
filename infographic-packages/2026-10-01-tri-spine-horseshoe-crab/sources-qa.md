## Evidence Lock

- IUCN check: [official assessment T21309A149768986](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2019-1.RLTS.T21309A149768986.en), *Tachypleus tridentatus*, scope **Global**, **Endangered (EN) A4bcd**, **Date Assessed 22 July 2018**, Year Published 2019. The page also has a separate 2026 Green Status assessment dated 11 December 2024; that date does not replace the Red List assessment year. Public footer uses 2018. No population total or threat rank is used.
- Accepted name: *Tachypleus tridentatus* (Leach, 1819). [ITIS](https://itis.gov/servlet/SingleRpt/SingleRpt?search_topic=TSN&search_value=0082705) records original combination *Limulus tridentatus* and English Japanese horseshoe crab / tri-spine horseshoe crab; [WoRMS](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=taxlist&tName=Limulus) maps *Limulus longispina* to the accepted name. Chinese horseshoe crab is another English alias in regional government usage. These aliases were included in the duplicate check.
- Japanese name and exact lineage: カブトガニ, Animalia > Arthropoda > Chelicerata > Xiphosurida > Limulidae > *Tachypleus*, according to [JAMSTEC BISMaL](https://www.godac.jamstec.go.jp/bismal/j/view/9003271). Editorial classification group: **Other invertebrates**. A horseshoe crab is a chelicerate rather than a decapod crustacean.
- Broad native region and habitat: East and Southeast Asian sheltered inshore coasts; sandy spawning beaches and neighboring intertidal nursery flats, supported by the [IUCN habitat account](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2019-1.RLTS.T21309A149768986.en) and [Japan Ministry of the Environment Setouchi account](https://www.env.go.jp/water/heisa/heisa_net/setouchiNet/seto/g1/g1chapter1/ikimono/kabutogani.html). The poster depicts a generic sheltered Asian bay, not a mapped locality.
- Discovery 1: eggs are laid on sandy beaches near the high tide line; nearby intertidal flats are juvenile nursery habitat. IUCN habitat account and Japan MOE section 6. Public card says larvae/young grow nearby, without implying every egg immediately moves on hatching.
- Discovery 2: three fixed spines are at the base of the tail spine and distinguish this exact species from the other living horseshoe crab species. [Kasaoka City Horseshoe Crab Museum](https://www.city.kasaoka.okayama.jp/site/kabutogani/3751.html), photographs and explanation around the answer image. Distinguish these from variable lateral marginal spines.
- Discovery 3: despite the common name, this species is in Chelicerata rather than Crustacea. JAMSTEC classification and [NCBI taxonomy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?id=6853). No unsupported claim that it is a spider is made.
- Emotional doorway: the beach and tidal flat form adjacent stages in one life story; the three spines reward a closer look.

- Diagnostic features: (1) broad smooth horseshoe-shaped front carapace, rounded leading edge [Iwaki full dorsal specimen, upper section]; (2) a visible hinge into a smaller posterior shield with a serrated/spiny lateral margin [Iwaki specimen, middle]; (3) a single long, straight, rigid tail spine centered on the rear shield, attached rather than detached [Iwaki lower section]; (4) three short fixed spines immediately around its base, separate from longer lateral marginal spines and not three tails [Kasaoka upper-left tile; user close-up and arrows]; (5) muted olive/tan carapace, no large crab pincers protruding around the dorsal silhouette [Iwaki specimen].
- Natural pose: dorsal three-quarter view on wet sand at a shallow tidal edge. Underside walking legs can be naturally hidden by the shields. A small card-2 close study may enlarge the tail-base structure, attached by a sketched connector line to the posterior shield. Card 1 uses sand/egg and juvenile-flat stages, not a second full-size hero.
- False silhouettes: not a true crab with a narrow abdomen and prominent pincers; not the Atlantic horseshoe crab or another Asian species lacking the three fixed spines; not a helmet-shaped turtle with a loose spear. Avoid three tail spines, extra eyes, a curved scorpion stinger, floating eggs, or detached limbs.
- Uncertainty: sex and lateral-spine number are left open. Reference photograph is a mounted dorsal specimen; do not make the wild individual stand upright like the mount.
