- Diagnostic features:
  1. Submerged rosette arising from one compact tuberous corm, with flexible petioles rather than a woody stem or creeping vine.
  2. Adult blades are long, oblong to narrowly oval and green, with a central midrib plus longitudinal and crosswise veins forming many repeated rectangular-to-oval openings.
  3. Openings occupy most of the mature blade interior while the outer margin remains continuous; the leaf is living green tissue, not a torn or decaying skeleton.
  4. Younger adult leaves are furled and may carry red-brown anthocyanin before holes open; mature blades are greener and visibly translucent.
  5. Natural scene is underwater freshwater with the corm anchored in streambed sediment and leaves bending gently with the current.
- Natural pose: three-quarter underwater view of the whole rosette. Two or three mature leaves fan toward the viewer, a younger furled leaf rises from the center, and some petiole bases may be naturally occluded by the rosette. The hero remains traceable to one corm.
- False silhouettes: not a terrestrial Monstera with broad heart-shaped lobed leaves; not dead skeletonized foliage; not branching seaweed, coral, fishing net or lace fabric; not another *Aponogeton* with a mostly solid wavy blade.
- Uncertainty: mature leaf width and lattice density vary among cultivated forms and developmental stages. The prompt locks the vein-grid logic and single-corm growth form, not an exact hole count.
