# Sources and QA — Cuban Gar

## Evidence Lock

- Accepted English common name: Cuban Gar
- Accepted scientific name: *Atractosteus tristoechus* (Bloch & Schneider, 1801)
- Japanese public title: キューバン・ガー
- Japanese naming caveat: no authoritative established Japanese common name was confirmed; the public title is a concise katakana rendering of the accepted English common name. Keep this caveat out of poster and main-post copy.
- Taxonomy: Actinopterygii; Lepisosteiformes; Lepisosteidae
- Broad native region: Central America/Caribbean
- Native range: western Cuba and Isla de la Juventud; the Cuban Red Data Book records Ciénaga de Zapata, Ciénaga de Lanier, and southern Pinar del Río.
- Habitat: shallow freshwater or brackish wetland waters with abundant aquatic vegetation; rivers, lakes, marshes, and estuarine water are documented across the official and specialist sources.
- Locked behavior: often remains quiet or moves little at mid-water, then moves rapidly when capturing prey; can use atmospheric oxygen when dissolved oxygen is low.
- Exact visual identity: elongated, robust, round/subcylindrical body; elongate but broad and relatively short flattened snout under 60 percent of head length; nostrils at the snout tip; two rows of enlarged fangs in the upper jaws; cheeks plated; large heavy rhomboid ganoid scales; dorsal and anal fins set far back just before a slightly asymmetrical rounded fan tail; dark gray-green back and lighter belly.
- Lookalike guard: do not render a narrow longnose-gar snout, an alligator-like crocodilian head, a forked tail, a teleost-style soft-scaled body, or extra dorsal/anal fins. The Cuban Gar's snout is broad and relatively short, and the dorsal and anal fins both attach far back near the tail.

## Image-facing QA lock

- Hero posture: safe side-profile or gentle three-quarter side view at mid-water; body nearly horizontal.
- Habitat: shallow Cuban wetland water, aquatic vegetation, muted green-brown water, a few surface-light cues.
- Required silhouette: robust elongated body; wide and relatively short flattened snout; dorsal and anal fins both far back near the tail; slightly asymmetrical rounded fan tail.
- Required surface: large dark gray-green rhomboid armor scales with a lighter underside.
- Mouth: closed or only slightly open; if teeth are visible, show two orderly upper-jaw rows without crocodilian exaggeration.
- Forbidden lookalikes/errors: narrow needle-like longnose-gar snout; broad crocodile skull; forked tail; duplicated fish; extra fins; scales replaced by smooth skin; bright spotted-gar pattern; fake map; prey attack scene; population graphics.
