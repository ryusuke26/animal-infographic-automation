## Visual identity

- Individual/stage: one adult hero; sex is not claimed. Several tiny complete juveniles appear only in Card 3.
- Reference roles: `evidence/brachiopsilus_dianthus_fishes_of_australia_reference.jpg` is the exact-taxon identity reference only, never an edit target; it controls live colour, smooth skin, eyes, lure, dorsal spines, fins and natural frontal posture. `evidence/pink_handfish_listing_statement_2023.pdf` controls official status context, rediscovery, depth, habitat and confusion risks. The rendered pages are inspection aids, not additional sources.
- Diagnostic features: (1) smooth naked pink to pink-red skin without wart-like protuberances; (2) reddish and pink blotches on head and body, darker red fins; (3) narrow illicium with a small rounded lure above the snout and two taller dorsal spines behind it; (4) two broad arm-like pectoral fins attached behind the head, with visible fin rays; (5) short tapering tail and long low dorsal fin.
- Natural pose: frontal three-quarter bottom-walk over rocky rubble. The near pectoral fin is fully readable; the far fin may be partly hidden by the head or reef. Pelvic fins remain small and subordinate.
- False silhouette: not Red Handfish, *Thymichthys politus* (no warty skin or fleshy protuberances); not typical Ziebell's Handfish, *Brachiopsilus ziebelli* (no bright yellow fins; pink and reddish rather than fawn/grey mottling); not a generic frogfish, frog or octopus (two ray-finned pectoral appendages attached to a fish body, not hands or arms).
- Uncertainty: the 2021–2022 deep-camera images can be low resolution and some probable records were not confirmed to species. The package uses the clear exact-taxon live photograph for anatomy and only the confirmed 2021 observation for public rediscovery wording.
