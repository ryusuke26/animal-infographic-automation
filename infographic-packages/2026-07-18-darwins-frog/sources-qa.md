## Visual identity guidance

- Show one small adult male Darwin's Frog on damp leaf litter or low moss in a cool southern-Chilean temperate forest.
- Diagnostic features: a small, slender frog; low triangular head with one pointed nasal projection at the very front of the snout; round lateral eyes; a smooth, low crown and back with no horn, spike, crest, or separate point above or behind either eye; short-to-moderate limbs; green, brown, or green-brown mottled back; dark belly with pale speckling only if it is naturally visible.
- Use a calm low crouch or side view that keeps the single pointed snout readable. The forehead must run back smoothly from the nasal point, as in the IUCN SSC Amphibian Specialist Group's side-view photograph: https://www.iucn-amphibians.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2020/07/image-2.png . The image must still read as a frog, not a horned frog, leaf, toad, lizard, or generic green tree frog.
- The three card icons may show a temperate-forest leaf/stream, a pointed leaf silhouette, and a gentle sound-bubble / tiny froglet cue. They must not show anatomical cutaways, eggs inside a mouth, exposed tadpoles, or extra adult frogs.
- Avoid: any supraocular horn, eye spike, cranial crest, raised triangular point behind the eye, generic tropical poison frog markings, red eyes, tree-frog toe pads, a large vocal sac balloon, a leaf with eyes, two adult frogs, a brood pouch cutaway, an aquarium, laboratory scene, fake map, nest, crowded tadpoles, people, rescue imagery, or printed claims beyond Copy Lock.
