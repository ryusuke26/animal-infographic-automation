## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Desmana moschata* (Linnaeus, 1758)
- English common name: Russian Desman
- Japanese common name: ロシアデスマン
- Broad native region: Europe
- Lineage: mole family, Talpidae; tribe Desmanini
- Native range: Russia, Ukraine, and Kazakhstan; chiefly the Don, Volga, and
  Ural river systems and tributaries
- Habitat: oxbow lakes, ponds, and slow-flowing rivers with abundant
  water-marsh vegetation and vegetated banks
- Global IUCN category: Critically Endangered (CR)
- Red List criteria: A2ac under version 3.1
- Scope: Global
- Assessment date: 29 March 2023
- Publication year: 2023
- Assessment identifier: `e.T6506A231334630`
- Official assessment DOI:
  https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2023-1.RLTS.T6506A231334630.en
- IUCN check: the user-supplied official IUCN species-page capture and matching
  13-page assessment PDF directly confirm Russian Desman / *Desmana moschata*,
  Global Critically Endangered (CR), criterion A2ac under version 3.1, assessed
  29 March 2023 and published in 2023. Both identify assessment
  `e.T6506A231334630`. The earlier record-rendering caveat is resolved. No

## Locked Public Claims

1. Habitat: plant-rich oxbow lakes, ponds, and slow-flowing waterways.
2. Visible identity: a rounded mole-relative body, long flexible snout, large
   webbed hind feet, and a laterally flattened tail.
3. Behavior: it swims underwater and probes the bottom for small aquatic prey.

Public copy omits population numbers, national legal categories, threat
rankings, blame, rescue framing, and unsupported exclusivity claims.

## Visual Identity Guidance

- Show exactly one adult Russian Desman in a natural horizontal swimming pose
  just below the surface of a vegetated oxbow lake or slow river margin.
- Rounded dark grey-brown body with dense glossy fur, very small eyes, no
  prominent external ear pinnae, a long narrow flexible proboscis-like snout,
  small clawed forefeet, much larger webbed paddle-like hind feet, and one long
  scaly tail that is thick at the base and laterally flattened toward the tip.
- Keep all four limbs visibly attached. The hind feet and tail must read as the
  main swimming surfaces; the snout should probe between submerged stems and
  stones without becoming a star-shaped nose or duck bill.
- Use a cutaway waterline with sedges, reeds, roots, a quiet burrow opening in
  the bank, submerged leaves, and muted green-brown water.

## Negative Constraints

- Not a muskrat, vole, rat, beaver, platypus, otter, shrew, star-nosed mole,
  elephant shrew, seal, or generic swimming rodent.
- No rodent incisors, broad beaver tail, duck bill, star-shaped nose, oversized
  ears, whisker fan replacing the snout, extra limbs, detached feet, duplicated
  tail, missing hind foot, extra animals, fish-school centerpiece, fake map,
  humans, fishing gear, logo, signature, or watermark.
