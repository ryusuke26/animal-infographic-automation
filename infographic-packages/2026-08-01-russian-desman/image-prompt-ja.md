# Japanese Direct Poster Prompt — Russian Desman

Create one complete true vertical 2:3 Japanese educational infographic poster,
designed as a handmade wetland naturalist's field-notebook cutaway around the
Russian Desman's long horizontal swimming silhouette. The bank, waterline,
hero, title, three observation cards, and footer must form one authored
composition, not three equal software panels.

## Native canvas requirement

Start from a completely new native `1024x1536` raster canvas. Fill the entire
canvas edge to edge with the warm-paper poster artwork. All four outermost
edges must contain intentional paper texture or background art; no white,
near-white, transparent, blank, padded, letterboxed, or pillarboxed strip is
allowed on any side. Place the hand-inked border evenly inside all four canvas
edges with balanced left and right margins. Do not place a narrower poster on
a wider canvas, and do not edit, extend, crop, pad, or image-reference a prior
poster.

## Subject and scene

Show exactly one identifiable adult Russian Desman, *Desmana moschata*, in a
natural horizontal swimming pose just below the surface of a quiet oxbow lake
or slow river margin in eastern Europe. Show a rounded dark grey-brown body
with dense glossy fur, very small eyes, no prominent external ear pinnae, a
long narrow flexible proboscis-like snout, two small attached clawed forefeet,
two much larger attached webbed paddle-like hind feet, and one long attached
scaly tail that is thick at the base and laterally flattened toward the tip.
The hero's snout reaches toward submerged stems and stones as it searches the
bottom. Keep all four limbs and the full tail visibly connected to the body.

Do not make a muskrat, vole, rat, beaver, platypus, otter, shrew, star-nosed
mole, elephant shrew, seal, or generic swimming rodent. No rodent incisors,
broad beaver tail, duck bill, star-shaped nose, oversized ears, whisker fan
replacing the snout, extra limbs, detached feet, hidden hind foot, duplicated
tail, extra animals, humans, fishing gear, fake map, logo, signature, or
watermark.

## Layout and visual hierarchy

Use warm fibrous paper, graphite, colored pencil, diluted gouache, and a
palette of reed green, peat brown, misty blue-grey, silvery bubbles, and muted
ochre. Build the composition around a curved cutaway waterline: sedges, reeds,
roots, and one quiet burrow opening above; submerged leaves, pebbles, and the
single swimming hero below. Let the long snout and flattened tail create a
gentle left-to-right flow from title to footer. Keep the area immediately
below the scientific name blank.

Place exactly three unequal irregular numbered observation cards in the
negative spaces around the swimming silhouette without covering the snout,
feet, body, or tail:

1. a small bank-and-water cutaway with dense marsh plants and a burrow opening;
2. a paired anatomical study of one complete webbed hind foot and the attached
   laterally flattened tail, clearly connected to a small body silhouette;
3. a close underwater vignette of the long flexible snout probing between
   stones and stems for small aquatic invertebrates.

Each card must have one clear number, one species-specific spot illustration,
and its full explanatory copy. No three equal boxes. Keep the hero dominant
and unobstructed. Integrate the quiet footer at the bottom. Make every line
legible on a phone.

## Text accuracy

Render only the following Japanese text exactly. Do not translate,
paraphrase, shorten, respell, repeat, or add words. Line wrapping is allowed
inside an item; preserve every character and order. Each observation appears
exactly once inside its numbered card.

Text, verbatim:
"ロシアデスマン"
"Desmana moschata"
"草が茂る流れの緩い水辺 岸の穴をねぐらにする"
"水かきの後ろ足、平たい尾 水中を進むモグラの仲間"
"長い鼻で水底をさぐる 水生の小さな生きものを探す"
"IUCN Red List 2023：深刻な危機（CR）"

Use clear correctly formed Japanese glyphs. Exactly three numbered illustrated
cards; no extra labels, captions, badges, decorative text, logos, or
watermarks.
