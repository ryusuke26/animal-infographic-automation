## Evidence route

The IUCN errata assessment record confirms Global Critically Endangered under C2a(i), assessed 26 July 2019, as `e.T80188449A185681031`. Biota of New Zealand marks *Hypocreopsis amplectens* as the current preferred name and supports its lineage and twig-clasping morphology. Royal Botanic Gardens Victoria and Fungimap support the English common name, damp long-unburnt woodland context, and growth over another wood-decay fungus. The Japanese display name is explicitly treated as an English-name transliteration rather than an established vernacular standard.

## Locked visual concept

One macro-scale cinnamon-brown fungus cluster grips one diagonal dead tea-tree twig in a damp, long-unburnt woodland field notebook. Exactly three unequal cards follow its identity from attached clasping lobes, to the fungus-on-fungus layer, to its southeastern Australia and New Zealand South Island setting. Spore-print texture, bark rubbings, and irregular paper notes are composed around the twig rather than placed in equal software panels.
