### Candidate 1 — Oceanic Hawaiian Damselfly / *Megalagrion oceanicum* — selected

- Global familiarity check: English exposure is concentrated in IUCN, US federal/state conservation, specialist odonate, and Hawai'i natural-resource pages; Japanese exact-name exposure is extremely sparse. It is not a recurring zoo, aquarium, global wildlife-video, viral, or generic weird-animal staple.
- Discovery doorway: a large red-orange damselfly begins as a stream naiad crawling through standing-wave riffles and can leave the water to forage on wet moss before the adult flies fast along the same current.
- Conservation doorway: the directly inspected current IUCN record `T12958A510839` is Global Critically Endangered (CR) under B1ab(iii,v), assessed 17 January 2019.
- Local-knowledge caution: public copy names O'ahu and the Ko'olau stream setting and does not claim that Hawai'i residents, Native Hawaiian communities, local researchers, or conservation teams do not know the species. The Hawaiian group word `pinapinao` is retained as context rather than presented as a species-specific name.
- Viability: the current IUCN page, USFWS profile/final rule, and Hawai'i DLNR page agree on the accepted name, large robust adult, red-orange male with black stripes, four clear wings, six legs, fast stream flight, and riffle/waterfall habitat. A high-resolution official adult photograph provides a direct visual reference.

## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted scientific name: *Megalagrion oceanicum* (McLachlan, 1883).
- English public name: Oceanic Hawaiian Damselfly, used by IUCN, USFWS, and Hawai'i DLNR.
- Japanese public display name: オセアニック・ハワイアン・ダムセルフライ. No standardized Japanese zoological name was found; this is a transparent katakana rendering of the authoritative English name. The caveat remains here rather than on the poster.
- Editorial classification group: Insects.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Arthropoda; Insecta; Odonata; Zygoptera; Coenagrionidae; *Megalagrion*.
- Broad native region: windward Ko'olau Mountains, O'ahu, Hawai'i / Oceania.
- Habitat: swiftly flowing sections of perennial montane streams, rock-and-gravel riffles, small cascades, and waterfall faces.
- Curiosity hook: an aquatic naiad works through standing waves and wet moss, then the red-orange adult patrols the same narrow stream corridor.

## Visual identity lock

- Reference role: the Hawai'i DLNR photograph by Dan Polhemus is a species-identity reference, never an edit target.
- Hero: exactly one complete adult male *Megalagrion oceanicum* perched naturally on a wet streamside twig above a fast green mountain stream; side view with head, thorax, abdomen, all four wings, and six connected legs readable.
- Adult body: comparatively large and robust for a damselfly; red-orange abdomen with narrow black marks, warm orange-red thorax with bold black longitudinal stripes, reddish compound eyes, and a dark terminal segment.
- Wings: exactly four transparent membranous wings, two on each side, folded together and back over the abdomen in a normal resting damselfly posture; continuous wing bases and fine venation.
- Legs: exactly six slender legs, each connected beneath the thorax and ending on the twig; natural overlap is allowed but no detached, duplicated, or spider-like limbs.
- Habitat: windward Ko'olau mountain stream with rock-and-gravel riffles, small standing waves, wet moss, fern shade, and a restrained waterfall-face cue. No ocean, beach, pond, aquarium, or tropical flower garden.
- Card art: card 1 shows a riffle/waterfall cross-section; card 2 shows one complete small perched adult, not isolated wings or legs; card 3 shows one complete elongated aquatic naiad crawling among gravel with three leaf-like terminal gills.
- False silhouettes to avoid: dragonfly with wings held flat, generic blue damselfly, *Megalagrion pacificum*, mosquito, mayfly, wasp, butterfly, spider, eight-legged insect, two-winged fly, thick clubbed abdomen, shortened abdomen, missing wing pair, or detached larval gills.
