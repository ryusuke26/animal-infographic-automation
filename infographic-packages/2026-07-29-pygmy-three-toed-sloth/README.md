## Topic Lock

- English common name: Pygmy Three-toed Sloth
- Japanese common name: ピグミーミツユビナマケモノ
- Scientific name: *Bradypus pygmaeus*
- Broad native region: Central America/Caribbean
- Lineage: three-toed sloth family, Bradypodidae
- Habitat: red mangroves and dense tropical forest on Isla Escudo de Veraguas,
  off Panama's Caribbean coast
- Curiosity hook: the world's smallest sloth can fade into island foliage with
  algae-green fur, then surprise the viewer by entering the water and swimming

## Image QA

- The initial Japanese and English posters were rejected after the user found
  a fifth hero limb gripping the underside of the diagonal branch.
- The first targeted edit erased only the terminal claw cluster while leaving
  the fifth limb-shaped appendage. The user correctly rejected it as a masked
  defect rather than a repair.
- A full side-on remake was then rejected locally for unnatural hindlimb
  construction. A later draft was rejected for a hidden/detached-looking
  forelimb.
- The user supplied a Gemini poster specifically as the natural pose
  reference. Its central sloth—not its text, maps, arrows, or decorative
  clutter—now defines the desired pelvis, tucked hind thighs, branch grip, and
  four-limb body mechanics.
- The user then authorized abandoning the repeated diagonal-branch layout.
  The current Japanese candidate is a fully new anatomy-first composition:
  one near-horizontal branch across the upper-middle, the hero suspended below
  it, two separated hindlimbs at the left/back, two separated forelimbs at the
  right/front, and four distinct grip points.
- The three unequal observation elements now occupy the negative spaces above,
  beside, and below the suspended silhouette. No prior poster layout is used;
  the Gemini image informs only natural body mass, fur, face, and joint
  softness.
- A prior layout-retaining Gemini-pose candidate was rejected before the full
  composition redesign.
- The user explicitly accepted the fully redesigned Japanese suspensory
  candidate. It became the canonical Japanese direct poster.
- The English companion was generated from the accepted Japanese poster and
  retained the same horizontal branch, suspended body, two separated
  hindlimbs at left/back, two separated forelimbs at right/front, four grip
  points, card concepts, palette, and hierarchy.
- Both language posters contain exactly the locked title, scientific name,
  three numbered illustrated observation texts, and quiet status footer. The
  English ASCII hyphens, colons, and `(CR)` spacing are correct.
- Both canonical direct posters and both posting PNGs are exact 1024x1536,
  true vertical 2:3, and each direct/posting pair is pixel-identical.
- At the user's request after final acceptance, ten rejected, superseded, or
  byte-duplicate PNGs were moved to the Windows Recycle Bin. The package now
  keeps only the four canonical poster PNGs; the user-supplied Gemini reference
  and official IUCN evidence remain.
