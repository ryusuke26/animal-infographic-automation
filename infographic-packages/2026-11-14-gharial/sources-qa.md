## Evidence Lock

- Exact taxon and names: *Gavialis gangeticus* (Gmelin, 1789). English: Gharial (also Indian gharial). Japanese: インドガビアル (common zoo/encyclopaedia name; short form ガビアル).
- Lineage: Animalia > Chordata > Reptilia > Crocodylia > Gavialidae > *Gavialis* > *G. gangeticus*.
- IUCN check: official record read directly at https://www.iucnredlist.org/species/8966/149227430 on 2026-11-14 — Global Critically Endangered A2bce, ver 3.1, Date Assessed 30 December 2017, Year Published 2019, citation Lang, J., Chowfin, S. & Ross, J.P. 2019 (errata version), e.T8966A149227430. Extant: Bangladesh, India, Nepal; extinct in Bhutan, Myanmar, Pakistan. Footer uses the assessment year 2017. Green Status (assessed 20 Mar 2025) is context only.
- Claim 1 (card 1): adult males develop a bulbous growth on the snout tip called a ghara after the Indian pot it resembles; it partially covers the nostrils, acts as a vocal resonator and is a visual signal to females (Smithsonian's National Zoo). Males grow it at sexual maturity; courtship visual stimulus (Animal Diversity Web).
- Claim 2 (card 2): 106–110 sharp teeth (ADW) → "100本余り / more than 100"; many sharp interlocking teeth, quickly striking at fish in water (Smithsonian); adults eat almost exclusively fish, snout becomes longer and thinner with age (ADW).
- Claim 3 (card 3): leg muscles are not strong enough to lift them off the ground (ADW); on land they push their bodies forward, "belly-sliding" (Smithsonian).
- Main post (beyond cards): males reach 5–6 m (Smithsonian); the knob is named after an Indian pot (Smithsonian).
- Story reply (beyond cards): females dig nests in the dry season, March–April, average 40 eggs; largest eggs of any crocodilian; females provide the sole parental care (Smithsonian); steep sandbanks (ADW); Chambal communal nest sites, adult females nest annually so nest numbers track reproductive females (IUCN account, ecological function section). Egg weight figures differ between sources (Smithsonian avg 160 g; ADW 100–156 g), so no weight is used.

## Visual identity brief

- Individual/stage: adult male (the ghara is male-only); vignette 3 shows a smaller gharial with no sex claim.
- Biological identity references (saved in references/, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0):
  1. `Gharial_Gavialis_gangeticus_male.jpg` — Charles J. Sharp, adult male in water: ghara shape/colour, teeth, head, dorsal scutes.
  2. `Gharial_male_and_female_NCS_India.jpg` — Hari Mohan Meena, National Chambal Sanctuary: male (dark knob) and female basking on a sandbar; whole-body proportions, low splayed posture, tail crest.
  3. `Gharial_Gavialis_gangeticus_female_head.jpg` — Charles J. Sharp: female head, snout length vs head width, needle teeth, raised eye, scale pattern.
- Diagnostic invariants: (1) extremely long, thin, straight snout, abruptly narrower than the broad head (refs 2, 3); (2) male: one dark lumpy rounded knob on top of the snout tip over the nostrils (refs 1, 2); (3) many small needle-like interlocking teeth along both jaws (refs 1, 3; ADW count); (4) olive-grey/grey-green back with keeled scutes, pale cream-yellow underside (ref 1; ADW colour); (5) low body on short splayed legs, long laterally flattened tail with double crest (ref 2).
- Natural pose: side view slightly from above, basking on a sandbar; far-side legs partly hidden by the body.
- False silhouettes: mugger/saltwater crocodile (broad snout); false gharial *Tomistoma* (no ghara, gradually tapering snout, reddish with dark bands); alligator.
