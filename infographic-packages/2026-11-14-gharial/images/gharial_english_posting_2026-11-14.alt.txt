Vertical poster in the style of an Indian natural-history painting, with fine outlines and stippling. Title and scientific name sit in a floral-bordered panel at the top. A male Gharial lies diagonally on a river sandbar: an extremely long, tube-like snout ending in a single lumpy grey-brown knob, rows of small needle-like teeth, an olive-grey body with rows of scales and a crested tail. Three numbered figures: 1, in a round frame on the right, a close-up of the knobbed snout tip beside a similar-shaped clay pot; 2, lower left, the slender jaws seizing a fish in the water; 3, lower right, a smaller gharial pushing forward on its belly across the sand, leaving a drag trail. Each has a heading and explanation. Footer: IUCN Red List 2017: Critically Endangered (CR).
