# Sources QA — Jade Vine / ヒスイカズラ

## Verdict

Overall: Ready for visual production. Accepted naming, the completed official no-assessment route, three public claims, and visual identity are locked.

## Candidate screen

- Hoatzin / *Opisthocomus hoazin* and Purple Frog / *Nasikabatrachus sahyadrensis* were rejected immediately as completed duplicates.
- Gharial / *Gavialis gangeticus* had viable official evidence, but its currently surfaced official materials require extra care around assessment versions and its long-jawed body plan carries greater visual risk.
- Peacock Mantis Shrimp / *Odontodactylus scyllarus* lacked a directly surfaced official global assessment and had weaker authoritative Japanese-name support.
- Jade Vine improves both rotation dimensions: Plants are absent from the latest eight completed packages, and its native Philippines/Asia region does not repeat yesterday's Central Australia/Oceania region.

## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Strongylodon macrobotrys* A.Gray.
- English common name: Jade Vine.
- Supported Japanese name: ヒスイカズラ（ジェイド・バイン）.
- Editorial classification group: Plants.
- Exact lineage: Plantae; Streptophyta; Equisetopsida; Magnoliidae; Fabales; Fabaceae; *Strongylodon*; *S. macrobotrys*.
- Native region and habitat: the Philippines in tropical Asia; a woody liana of wet tropical rainforest, including damp forest along streams or ravines.
- Naming caveats: Kew lists *Strongylodon megaphyllus* Merr. and *Strongylodon warburgii* Perkins as heterotypic synonyms; the public identity line uses the accepted name.
- IUCN check: exact searches of the current IUCN Red List route under the accepted name and both Kew-listed synonyms did not surface a global species assessment on 2026-08-07. Kew's current Jade Vine page explicitly displays IUCN Red List status `Not Evaluated`.
- Publication-safe status wording: `IUCN世界評価は確認できず（2026年確認）` / `No global IUCN assessment confirmed (checked 2026)`. Public copy does not present `IUCN NE` as if it were a formal assessment.

## Visual identity guidance

- One dominant woody liana visibly connected from climbing stem to trifoliate leaves and one long hanging flower truss.
- Natural green leaves with three leaflets; not five leaflets and not a compound wisteria look.
- Many thick, waxy blue-green to jade flowers, each with a strongly curved double-hook silhouette.
- Humid Philippine tropical rainforest with mossy bark, filtered canopy light, and damp stream/ravine cues; not a greenhouse or garden trellis.
- Card 2 shows one attached flower with its short stalk; card 3 shows one complete small bat naturally visiting a flower, never isolated or floating anatomy.
- Not wisteria, grapes, orchids, lotus, a succulent, teal glass, jade jewelry, a freestanding shrub, or a generic turquoise bloom.

## Discovery doorway

After dark beneath a Philippine rainforest canopy, a long blue-green truss hangs like a cluster of curved jade claws. A nectar-feeding bat touches pollen with its head and carries the flower's night story onward.
