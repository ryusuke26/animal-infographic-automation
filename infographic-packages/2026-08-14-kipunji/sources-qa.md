## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Rungwecebus kipunji* (Ehardt, Butynski, Jones & Davenport, 2005).
- Taxonomic caveat: the species was originally described as *Lophocebus kipunji*; later morphological and molecular work established the monotypic genus *Rungwecebus*. Use the accepted combination publicly.
- English common name: Kipunji. `Highland Mangabey` is a historical/alternate English name and is not used as the title.
- Supported Japanese name: キプンジザル, as used in a peer-reviewed Japanese primatology paper.
- Editorial classification group: Mammals.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Mammalia; Primates; Cercopithecidae; Cercopithecinae; Papionini; *Rungwecebus*; *R. kipunji*.
- Broad native region: southern Tanzania / Africa.
- Range and habitat: discrete submontane and montane forests in Tanzania's Southern Highlands and Udzungwa Mountains, about 1,300–2,450 m elevation. Public copy uses the conservative phrase `southern Tanzania's montane forests`.
- Global IUCN category: Endangered (EN), version 3.1.
- Assessment date/year: 20 March 2018; public footer year `2018`.
- Publication year: 2019; retained only in source/citation context.
- Assessment identifier: `e.T136791A17961368`.
- Official assessment DOI: https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2019-3.RLTS.T136791A17961368.en
- IUCN check: the official assessment PDF page 2 and matching Red List page capture directly show Global Endangered (EN), criteria `B1ab(ii,iii,iv,v)+2ab(ii,iii,iv,v)`, assessed 20 March 2018 and published in 2019. PDF page 3 lists 2008 CR only as a previous assessment. MDD and 2026 peer-reviewed usage independently agree on current EN; the WCS Tanzania CR wording is not used for current status.
- Check date: 2026-08-14.

## Visual identity guidance

- Show one adult Kipunji as a medium-large arboreal monkey with a shaggy light-to-medium gray-brown coat, dark face and black eyelids, long cheek whiskers, a broad erect crown crest, dark lower limbs/hands/feet, a paler central belly, and a long tail whose distal half is pale off-white.
- Stable pose: pause or low quadrupedal stance on a broad mossy branch. Both shoulders and hips are visible; each arm and leg has a separate path and endpoint with negative space from its near/far counterpart. Both hands and both feet contact the branch separately.
- The tail begins at the rear pelvis, extends behind the body, and keeps its pale tip visible. It must not emerge from the back, cross the torso, split, curl like a pig tail, or vanish behind a card.
- Card 2 uses one complete small side-view Kipunji to show both the erect crest and pale tail tip. Card 3 uses one complete calling animal with a subtle sound-wave mark, not a floating head or detached mouth.
- Not a generic baboon, macaque, langur, guenon, or black mangabey. No hairless baboon muzzle, bright colored rump, white eyelids, ringed tail, red face, golden coat, oversized canine display, extra limbs, or duplicated hero.

## Discovery doorway

Mist hangs in a southern Tanzanian mountain forest. A shaggy brown monkey slips behind wet leaves; its pale tail tip disappears, but a low honk-bark continues through the canopy. The animal first described in 2005 would soon anchor a newly named African monkey genus.
