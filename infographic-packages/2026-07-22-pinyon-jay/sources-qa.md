## Evidence Lock

- Accepted English common name: Pinyon Jay
- Accepted scientific name: *Gymnorhinus cyanocephalus* Wied, 1841
- Japanese public title: マツカケス
- Japanese naming basis: the Ornithological Society of Japan journal article uses `マツカケス Gymnorhinus cyanocephalus`. `https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjo/68/1/68_67/_pdf`
- Taxonomy: Animalia; Chordata; Aves; Passeriformes; Corvidae; *Gymnorhinus*. Official IUCN PDF page 2 records the accepted name and authority.
- Broad native region: North America
- Native range: western North America, especially the interior western United States and adjoining parts of Mexico; public copy stays at the broad western North America level.
- Habitat: pinyon–juniper woodland and related dry woodland, with pinyon pine seeds central to foraging and caching.
- Locked behavior: highly social flocks forage together; the long pointed bill extracts pinyon seeds; birds cache seeds in soil, and unretrieved seeds can germinate.
- Exact visual identity: medium-small jay; overall dull or dusty blue; paler blue belly; small whitish chin/throat; no crest; short tail; long straight sharply pointed dark bill; two folded wings and two legs only.
- Lookalike guard: not a Blue Jay, Steller's Jay, California Scrub-Jay, Clark's Nutcracker, magpie, raven, or generic tropical blue bird. No crest, black necklace, white wing bars, black hood, long graduated tail, gray-black-white nutcracker pattern, or extra bill/horn projection.
