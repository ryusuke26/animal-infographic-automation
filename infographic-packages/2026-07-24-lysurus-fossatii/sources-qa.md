- Japanese naming caveat: no established Japanese common name was confirmed. The public title is a concise katakana rendering of the accepted scientific name; the caveat stays in this QA file rather than on the poster or main post.
- Taxonomy: Fungi; Basidiomycota; Agaricomycetes; Phallales; Phallaceae.
- Taxonomic source cited by the IUCN assessment: Index Fungorum Partnership (2023).

### Native range and broad region

- Broad region: South America.
- Native country: Argentina.
- Known range: two areas in Argentina—around Estancia El Yucat in Córdoba Province and Sierra de la Ventana in Buenos Aires Province.
- Public wording: “known from two areas in Argentina,” without converting collections or inferred population size into a population claim.

### Habitat

- Córdoba records: wet, mulch-rich soil in shade among shrubs and trees in Espinal forest.
- Buenos Aires record: grassland in an area exposed to sun in Sierra de la Ventana.
- The IUCN habitat scheme lists subtropical/tropical dry forest and subtropical/tropical dry grassland as suitable.
- Public wording: rainy-season emergence from soil in dry forest or grassland.

### Behavior and distinctive traits

1. The immature fruit body is egg-like, enclosed by a light-yellow outer layer marked by a seam-like network.
2. The mature fruit body is unusually simple for *Lysurus*: a short cream-white hollow stalk supports a dark-green to dark-brown fertile mass, without the arms or cage seen in many lookalike stinkhorns.
3. In laboratory observations, emergence and elongation occurred mainly at night; the dark gleba then liquefied and released an unpleasant odor described as resembling yeast or decayed peaches.

The public copy does not repeat the paper's striking elongation-rate measurement because it comes from limited laboratory observations and is not needed for the story.

## Three core public claims

1. Light-yellow, seam-patterned “egg” stage.
2. Cream-white simple stalk topped by a dark-green fertile mass, without arms or a cage.
3. Mainly nighttime emergence in laboratory observations, followed by liquefaction and a yeast/decayed-peach-like odor.

All three are supported by the 2022 peer-reviewed original description. The range, habitat, and Global VU status are independently supported by the 2023 IUCN assessment.

## Exact visual identity guidance

- Depict one mature fruit body and one small egg-stage spot icon only; the egg is an explanatory icon, not a second hero specimen.
- Mature body: short, thick, upright cream-white hollow stalk, up to about 5.5 cm tall and 2 cm wide in the description.
- Fertile portion: a compact globose-to-cylindrical cap-like mass, up to about 1 cm high and 2.5 cm wide, dark green becoming dark brown; it sits directly on the top of the stalk.
- Base: irregular remnants of a light-yellow egg-like outer layer may remain at soil level.
- Habitat: mulch-rich soil and leaf litter with Espinal shrubs/grasses after rain.
- Do not add a mushroom cap, gills, arms, tentacles, a lattice/cage, a red-orange crown, branching antler shapes, a skirt, a veil, multiple stalks, or an oversized tall stem.
- Do not depict flies, animals, or visible spores. No anatomy cutaway.
- Visual reference hierarchy: the peer-reviewed original-description photographs and dimensions first; generic *Lysurus* or stinkhorn images are not identity references.
