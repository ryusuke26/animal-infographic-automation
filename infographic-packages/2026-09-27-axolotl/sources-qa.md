## Evidence Lock

IUCN check: matching formal assessment text for record T1095A53947343 confirms Global Critically Endangered (CR), Date Assessed 23 October 2019, Year Published 2020; the official dynamic page was inaccessible in this run, so the PDF text mirror and DOI were cross-checked.

- Accepted taxon: *Ambystoma mexicanum* (Shaw & Nodder, 1798), Amphibia > Caudata > Ambystomatidae; AMNH Amphibian Species of the World confirms current name. Historical combination: *Gyrinus mexicanus*. English Axolotl; Japanese メキシコサンショウウオ (Japan Ministry of Environment usage); ウーパールーパー is a familiar Japanese colloquial alias. Spanish ajolote is recognized without treating Mexican audiences as unfamiliar with it.
- Native region/habitat: Valley of Mexico, especially the Xochimilco freshwater lake/canal system. The formal IUCN assessment also records Chalco and Chapultepec sites; avoid a false claim of only one current water body. The scene is a Xochimilco-like aquatic margin, not a generic aquarium.
- Global status: IUCN record T1095A53947343, Critically Endangered (CR), criterion A2abce, **Date Assessed 23 October 2019**; Year Published 2020. Use 2019 in the footer. No population total or legal category is asserted.
- Claim 1: wild-type axolotls are dark/mottled brown, unlike common pale captive forms — New England Aquarium and Lincoln Park Zoo. The card describes the animal's appearance; no universal color claim.
- Claim 2: adults retain feathery external gills and remain aquatic — New England Aquarium and Lincoln Park Zoo.
- Claim 3: axolotls can regenerate a missing limb — Lincoln Park Zoo and Smithsonian Q?rius. Avoid an instantaneous or guaranteed-perfect healing claim.
- Discovery doorway: the familiar pale animal has a dark wild-type counterpart living underwater with adult gills.

## Compact visual identity brief

- Individual/stage: one mature wild-type animal; sex unspecified. No pale leucistic morph. Show no captive tank or handling.
- Exact-taxon identity references: [New England Aquarium wild-type discussion and captioned female MC/Fantasma photographs](https://www.neaq.org/axolotls-at-the-aquarium/) for dark mottled body, head and external gill appearance; [Lincoln Park Zoo species account](https://www.lpzoo.org/animals/axolotl/) for aquatic adult anatomy and dark wild color; [CONABIO exact-taxon image record](https://bdi.conabio.gob.mx/fotoweb/archives/5007-Anfibios/Animales/Vertebrados/Anfibios/LCF0404%20Ambystoma%20mexicanum.jpg.info) for species identity. Remote image download was blocked by sandbox socket permissions, so the supplied prompt uses these as named biological descriptions rather than local attachments.
- Defining features: (1) broad, flattened salamander head with small eyes and wide mouth; (2) three feathery external gill stalks on each side immediately behind the head, attached rather than crown-like detached plumes; (3) elongated dark olive-brown mottled trunk, no pink or white captive body; (4) four short attached legs with natural far-side occlusion; (5) continuous laterally flattened finned tail from the trunk, not a separate fish tail. The zoo descriptions and visible exact-taxon photos ground each region.
- Natural pose: one three-quarter side view moving gently among submerged plants; far-side limbs and gills may be partly hidden by head/body and water plants. Do not force all limbs into an unnaturally splayed pose.
- False silhouettes: no pink pet axolotl, generic frog tadpole, fish with detached gills, or salamander without external gills. Card 3 is an attached limb-sequence study, never a free-floating extra limb on the hero.
- Uncertainty: exact speckle pattern and sex are variable, so do not lock a fixed marking map or genital anatomy.
