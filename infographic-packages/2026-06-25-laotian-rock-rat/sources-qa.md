## Source Disagreements And Caveats

- Taxonomy caveat: the original description placed the species in a new family, Laonastidae. Later fossil and molecular work connected it to Diatomyidae. Public copy says "a lineage once known from fossils" and avoids an over-technical family claim on the poster.
- Conservation status caveat: PLOS One 2012 notes a 2009 Endangered listing. Later IUCN indexing records a 2016 Least Concern assessment by Duckworth. The footer uses the later 2016 LC assessment and records the older status as superseded.
- Range caveat: publications and secondary summaries report Lao PDR and western Viet Nam records. Poster copy uses "Laos limestone karst" to avoid over-mapping a small and complex range.

## Visual Identity Guidance

Show one Laotian rock rat in limestone karst habitat:

- Dark slate-gray to brown-gray rodent body.
- Rounded ears, black bead-like eyes, long whiskers.
- Short legs and slightly splayed feet on angular limestone rocks.
- Thick furry tail, not a thin naked rat tail and not an upright squirrel tail.
- Quiet karst vegetation, moss, leaf litter, and pale limestone blocks.
- Natural low scrambling posture on rocks.

Avoid:

- Generic laboratory rat, house mouse, squirrel, pika, rabbit, mole, lemur, or possum.
- Long naked pink tail.
- Huge front teeth, cartoon pest cues, cages, markets, traps, maps, fossil skeletons, museum cases, or population graphics.
- Multiple specimens or anatomy insets.
