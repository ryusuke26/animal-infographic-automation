- Diagnostic features:
  1. Adult has a wide, soft-looking central disc with 15–24 flexible tapered arms; the hero should show about 20 connected arms rather than a generic five-point star.
  2. Arms radiate continuously from the disc with no brittle-star joints, detached rays or duplicated origins; individual rays may curve and overlap naturally over rock.
  3. Dorsal surface is low and pliable with fine pale spines or mottling; colour may be orange, ochre, purple-brown or a natural combination, never hard glossy armor.
  4. Underside tube feet are small repeated soft projections in grooves along the arms; Card 2 may reveal them in a connected underside detail without turning them into long tentacles.
  5. The animal stays in full contact with a rocky or mixed kelp-forest seafloor; no rigid upright star pose or floating radial emblem.
- Natural pose: dorsal three-quarter view of one adult crawling across a rock-and-gravel kelp-forest floor. Most arms are visible; a few far-side tips may pass behind kelp or rock. Natural overlap is allowed, but every visible arm must trace back to the central disc.
- False silhouettes: not a generic five-armed sea star; not a brittle star with a tiny disc and sharply jointed whip arms; not a crown-of-thorns starfish with heavy venomous spines; not a rigid sunburst badge.
- Uncertainty: adult arm count and colour vary. Do not imply that every adult has exactly 24 arms, and do not force hidden arm tips into view merely to display a count.
