## Compact visual identity brief
Individual: adult exact taxon, no sex-specific body ratio or palp assertion; no male/female size comparison. Female egg-laying implicit in card3; both sexes build bells.
References: references/water-spider-1.jpg and water-spider-2.jpg, official Tokyo Zoological Park Society, full-body oblique/ventral angles, identity only. references/bell-study-figure1.jpg, Neumann & Kureck2013 CC-BY2.0, panels A/B for anchored bell and lower interface only. Other panels microscopy/lab setup not copied into art. Three role-distinct images, no gallery export.
Five defining features:
1. Two joined body regions: reddish/dark brown anterior prosoma/sternum and oval dark grey-brown abdomen, narrow connecting waist — both zoo photos central body.
2. Four pairs of long jointed brown walking legs attach to prosoma, no walking appendages from abdomen — both photo side origins/leg trajectories; short mouth palps distinct, natural far-leg overlap allowed.
3. Fine hairs especially abdominal and rear-leg surfaces hold a silver reflective air coating, not a detached balloon/metal shell — bright abdominal edge and grey central sheen in both zoo photos plus araneae description.
4. Oblique plant-clinging underwater pose, natural flexed legs — zoo2 diagonal suspended grip; no forced radial leg display or water-strider surface standing.
5. Bell separate from body plastron, silk anchored between supports with rounded roof and lower horizontal interface/open underside — research Fig1B, 2013 open-bottom discussion; no sealed glass sphere or top-opening bowl.
Natural occlusion: far legs/short palps may overlap body or weed; in bell detail some limbs hidden by air and silk. Never bend body to expose all legs. Dark eyes not enlarged; no speculative eye-count diagram. No internal feeding aperture anatomy.
False silhouettes: diving beetle has wing cases and six legs; water strider sits on surface and has insect segments; tarantula has heavy hairy proportions absent here. Do not turn air sheen into a spherical abdomen.
Uncertainty: sex not identified in zoo photos, so no diagnostic sex label. Diagram arrows indicate gas transfer, not literal oxygen bubbles or flow. Detail sizes qualitative.
