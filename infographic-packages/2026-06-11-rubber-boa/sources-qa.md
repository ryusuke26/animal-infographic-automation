## Visual Identity Guidance

Show one compact brown or olive-brown rubber boa in a damp western North American forest-floor setting. It has a uniformly thick, tube-like body with small smooth glossy scales, a blunt wedge-shaped head no wider than the neck, tiny dark eyes, and a distinctly short rounded tail. Use a loose protective coil with the real head tucked partly inward and the blunt tail gently raised at the outer edge. Add flat stones, a rotting log, leaf litter, moss, and a subtle nearby stream or moist woodland cue.

Avoid a rattlesnake rattle, triangular viper head, neck constriction, hood, fangs, open striking mouth, bright bands, python blotches, long tapering tail, two-headed snake, giant tropical constrictor, desert-only scene, human handling, fake map, or rescue imagery. Do not depict the Southern Rubber Boa, *Charina umbratica*, as the named species.
