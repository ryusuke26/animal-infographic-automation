# Greater Hog Badger Evidence and Claim QA

## Verdict

Overall: Ready for a Quality Run. The official IUCN global assessment PDF and species-record screenshot directly confirm Vulnerable (VU), criteria A2cd+3cd+4cd, assessed 3 March 2015.

IUCN check: confirmed via the official global assessment record.

Publication context: the assessment was originally published in 2016. IUCN published an amended version in 2024 solely to correct the assessor name from `Choudhury, A.` to `Choudhury, A.U.`; the category, criteria, and assessment date did not change.

## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Arctonyx collaris* (F. Cuvier, 1825).
- English common name: Greater Hog Badger.
- Supported Japanese name: ブタバナアナグマ.
- Native region: Asia; Bangladesh and northeast India east through mainland Southeast Asia, with probable occurrence in Yunnan, China.
- Lineage: order Carnivora, family Mustelidae, subfamily Melinae.
- Habitat: grassland, hills, mountains, and tropical evergreen or semi-evergreen forest; public copy conservatively says forests.
- Global IUCN category/year: Vulnerable (VU), criteria A2cd+3cd+4cd, assessed 3 March 2015; originally published in 2016 and amended in 2024.
- Curiosity doorway: a stocky forest badger that lowers a long pig-like snout to the leaf litter and follows it with pale digging claws.

## Naming and status notes

- The Mammal Science world-mammal standard-name list pairs *Arctonyx collaris* with ブタバナアナグマ.
- MDD treats *A. collaris* as a valid species and notes that *A. albogularis* and *A. hoevenii* were formerly included. Public range and visual guidance therefore follow the narrower modern species concept and exclude Sumatra.
- The official assessment distinguishes the field-level assessment date, 3 March 2015, from the original 2016 publication and the 2024 amended publication. Public footers correctly use the assessment year 2015.
- The 2024 amendment changes only one assessor's initials. It does not change the species, scope, category, criteria, date assessed, or population trend.
- The official assessment describes the species as active throughout day and night. The night scenes in the story copy remain accurate but do not imply exclusive nocturnality.
- No population estimate, trend, ranked threat, legal status, or urgency slogan is used publicly.

## Visual identity guidance

- One low, stocky mustelid with a long pig-like pinkish-brown snout, small rounded ears, coarse gray-brown coat, a broad pale head and throat, and two dark stripes running from the snout through the eyes toward the neck.
- Four short powerful legs. Each shoulder or hip origin, limb path, and paw endpoint must remain visible and separate. Foreclaws are long and pale; hind paws are separate and grounded.
- One short pale or yellowish tail connected at the rump.
- Stable three-quarter side view with the nose just above leaf litter; the hero is walking or pausing, not twisting, rearing, or buried in foliage.
- Not a European Badger, American Badger, skunk, raccoon, civet, bear cub, pig, or generic striped mammal. No short blunt badger muzzle, panda-like face, ringed tail, hooves, extra stripes, extra limbs, hidden paws, or detached anatomy.
