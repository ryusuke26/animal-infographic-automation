- Global familiarity check: Japanese exposure is extremely sparse and usually concerns the much more familiar African Clawed Frog; English exposure is concentrated in specialist amphibian, Cameroon conservation, and a small number of zoo-breeding pages rather than recurring global wildlife, aquarium, viral-video, or generic weird-animal culture.

- Local-knowledge caution: public copy names Lake Oku and Cameroon and introduces this lake life to distant viewers without claiming that Oku communities, Cameroonian researchers, or local conservation teams do not know it.

## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted scientific name: *Xenopus longipes* Loumont & Kobel, 1991.
- English public name: Lake Oku Clawed Frog. Amphibian Species of the World also records Savannah Clawed Frog, but Lake Oku Clawed Frog is the current IUCN and EAZA name and directly anchors the habitat.
- Japanese public display name: オク湖のツメガエル. No standardized Japanese zoological name was confirmed; this is a transparent rendering of Lake Oku Clawed Frog. The caveat remains here rather than on the poster.
- Editorial classification group: Amphibians.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Amphibia; Anura; Pipidae; Dactylethrinae; *Xenopus*.
- Broad native region: Lake Oku, Cameroon Highlands, Northwest Region, Cameroon / Africa.
- Habitat: clear freshwater in the high-elevation volcanic crater lake Lake Oku, surrounded by montane cloud forest, with submerged aquatic vegetation and leaf-silt margins.
- Curiosity hook: a tiny adult with remarkably long webbed feet shares one crater lake with a translucent, barbel-bearing tadpole more than twice its body length.

## Visual identity lock

- Hero: exactly one complete adult *Xenopus longipes* cruising naturally in clear shallow Lake Oku water. Stable side-to-dorsal three-quarter view, not a dramatic leap, twisted turn, or symmetrical specimen display.
- Adult body: small, pear-shaped and slightly flattened; dark brown to warm orange mottling above; grey underside with dense black speckling where visible; eyes set high; a short subocular tentacle below each eye.
- Forelimbs: the near forelimb is readable with four long unwebbed fingers and no claws; the far forelimb may be partly hidden by the head and chest in natural perspective. Occlusion is preferable to inventing or unnaturally exposing a limb.
- Hind limbs: the near long hind limb and complete foot are readable, with five long toes joined by a broad translucent web and exactly three dark inner claw tips. The far hind limb may overlap the torso naturally. Anatomical continuity matters more than displaying both feet.
- Tadpole: translucent oval head-body, large dark eyes, very long narrow tail, tiny paired mouth-corner barbels, and no adult feet unless depicting a clearly developed complete metamorphic stage.
- Habitat: cool clear olive-teal crater-lake water, submerged vegetation, restrained leaf silt, and filtered cloud-forest light. No aquarium glass, coral, tropical reef, generic pond lilies, map, or flag.
- False silhouettes to avoid: ordinary African Clawed Frog, generic tree frog, toad, poison frog, bullfrog, salamander, fish, axolotl, tadpole with a round short tail, front-foot claws, unwebbed hind toes, four or five hind claws, extra or detached limbs, or a forced symmetrical all-limbs-visible pose.
