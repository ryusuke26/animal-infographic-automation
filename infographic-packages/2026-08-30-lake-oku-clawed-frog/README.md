## Locked scope

- Accepted name: *Xenopus longipes* Loumont & Kobel, 1991.
- English public name: Lake Oku Clawed Frog; Savannah Clawed Frog is retained only as a recorded alias.
- Japanese public name: オク湖のツメガエル. No standardized Japanese zoological name was confirmed; this is a transparent Japanese rendering of the authoritative English common name, and the caveat stays in evidence context.
- Global status: IUCN Red List Critically Endangered (CR) under B1ab(iii,v)+2ab(iii,v), assessed 6 February 2017 and published as an amended assessment in 2020, record `T58176A177346697`.
- Public claims: it is fully aquatic and native to high-elevation Lake Oku; the small pear-shaped adult has long digits, fully webbed hind feet, and three dark claws on each hind foot; its translucent tadpole can grow more than twice the adult body length.
- Discovery doorway: beneath the cloud forest around one volcanic crater lake, a tiny long-footed frog shares the clear water with a tadpole much longer than the adult.

## Production result

- Earlier candidates over-exposed every limb and translated fine surface variation into raised-looking texture. At the user's request, the two package images and three matching generated originals were moved to the Windows Recycle Bin before the restart.
- The restarted workflow first established a text-free visual anchor. Natural occlusion is treated as correct anatomy: the near limbs remain readable while far limbs overlap the body instead of being forced into a symmetrical specimen pose. Two discarded anchor intermediates were also moved to the Windows Recycle Bin; only the accepted anchor remains in the package.
- The frog body uses broad, calm watercolor and matte-gouache fields. Fine pencil texture is confined to habitat and card borders, so the skin reads as a smooth continuous plane at full and phone size.
- The Japanese poster was generated fresh from the accepted visual anchor. The English companion was generated fresh from the accepted Japanese poster and anchor. Neither is an edit of a rejected poster.
- Both direct posters pass the exact-2:3/full-canvas source gate. Both posting PNGs are exact `1024x1536` and pixel-identical to their accepted direct source within language.
