## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted public scientific name: *Hadramphus tuberculatus* (Pascoe, 1877), used by IUCN and New Zealand's Department of Conservation.
- Historical duplicate names: original combination *Lyperobius tuberculatus* Pascoe, 1877; historical combination *Karocolens tuberculatus* (Pascoe, 1877).
- English public names: Canterbury Knobbled Weevil; Canterbury Knobbed Weevil; Banks Peninsula Speargrass Weevil; Spaniard Weevil.
- Japanese public name: カンタベリー・コブゾウムシ, a transparent rendering of the official English name. No standardized Japanese insect-list name was confirmed.
- Editorial classification group: Insects.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Arthropoda; Insecta; Coleoptera; Curculionidae; *Hadramphus*.
- Broad native region: Canterbury, South Island, Aotearoa New Zealand / Oceania.
- Habitat: dry Canterbury grassland and high-country speargrass patches, especially plants in the genus *Aciphylla*.
- Curiosity hook: after roughly eighty years without a record, the species was still walking on speargrass.

## Official status lock

- IUCN check: directly inspected on the current official species page in Red List version 2026-1; checked 2026-09-10.
- Record: `T39307A21424332`; DOI `10.2305/IUCN.UK.2014-1.RLTS.T39307A21424332.en`.
- Scope and category: Global Critically Endangered (CR), criteria B1ab(v)+2ab(v), version 3.1.
- Date assessed: 19 December 2013. Year published: 2014. Public copy uses the assessment year 2013.
- Annotation: `Needs updating`. The 2013 page reports one location and 138 mature individuals; both values are excluded from public copy because DOC confirmed a second population in December 2024 and says its size and security are not yet known.

## Visual identity

- Individual/stage: one adult exact-taxon weevil; sex is not assigned. The larval stage appears only as a small root-section diagram in Card 2, not as an identity reference.
- Reference roles: `evidence/hadramphus_tuberculatus_iucn_lateral_reference.jpg` controls side silhouette, rostrum, antennae and stance. `evidence/hadramphus_tuberculatus_iucn_close_reference.jpg` controls scaling, mottling, elytral ridges and knobs. `evidence/hadramphus_tuberculatus_doc_dorsal_reference.jpg` controls the broader dorsal silhouette on speargrass.
- Diagnostic features: (1) large stout oval weevil rather than a slender grain weevil; (2) grey-brown scales with irregular pale mottling; (3) hard fused wing cases with thick longitudinal ridges and many rounded tubercles; (4) short broad downward-curving rostrum, not a long needle; (5) one pair of elbowed antennae plus six robust walking legs.
- Natural pose: one adult walking lengthwise along a speargrass blade in dorsal three-quarter view. The far legs may be partly hidden by the body or leaf; no limb is forced into view and the wings stay closed.
- False silhouette: not a smooth grain weevil; not a long-snouted nut weevil; not a horned scarab; not an eight-legged arachnid; not an open-wing flying beetle.
- Uncertainty: the exact sex, individual size and visibility of all six feet are not assigned from the photographs. Hidden legs are acceptable, but every visible limb and antenna must attach coherently.
