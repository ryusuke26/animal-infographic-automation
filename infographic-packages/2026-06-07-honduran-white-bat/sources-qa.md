# Sources and QA - Honduran White Bat

## Verdict

Overall: Ready with naming context and conservative diet wording.

## Evidence Lock

- Accepted common name: Honduran White Bat.
- Japanese display name: シロヘラコウモリ, a widely circulated Japanese rendering; no authoritative Japanese standard-name list was located in this search.
- Scientific name: *Ectophylla alba* H. Allen, 1892.
- Taxonomy: Mammalia, Chiroptera, Phyllostomidae, Stenodermatinae.
- Native region: Caribbean-slope lowlands from Honduras through Nicaragua and Costa Rica to western or northwestern Panama.
- Habitat: wet lowland evergreen and secondary forest with suitable understory leaves for roost tents.
- Status footer: IUCN Red List 2015: Near Threatened (NT).
- Core claim 1: individuals modify broad understory leaves, especially *Heliconia*, into inverted-boat tents used as roosts.
- Core claim 2: the fur is white; ears, nose-leaf, and other bare skin are yellow, with lutein documented in the skin.
- Core claim 3: it is a fruit-eating bat strongly associated with figs; public copy does not claim an exclusive one-species diet.

## Image Identity Guidance

- Small leaf-nosed bat hanging upside down with wings folded close to the body.
- Dense snow-white fur; warm yellow ears, nose-leaf, lips, and wing digits/bare skin.
- Two distinct triangular ears and a compact yellow leaf-shaped nose structure.
- Dark eyes; no exaggerated fangs.
- Roost beneath a broad green leaf folded into an inverted-boat tent.
- Tropical wet lowland forest understory, softly lit daytime shade.
- One to three bats are acceptable, but one clearly readable individual is safest.
- Avoid a mouse sitting on a leaf, an albino generic bat, a bird, moth, plush toy, fruit with a face, black wings spread in flight, or a sealed leaf cocoon.

## Uncertainty Notes

- The 2015 IUCN assessment remains the latest formal global assessment located for this package. The year is shown explicitly.
- Country and range descriptions vary between "western" and "northwestern" Panama; public copy uses western Panama.
- Leaf-tent studies are primarily from Costa Rica and should not be phrased as proof that every roost everywhere uses the same plant species.
- The yellow-skin claim refers to carotenoid deposition documented in sampled bats; the poster keeps the wording visual and does not turn it into a medical claim.
