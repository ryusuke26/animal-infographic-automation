- Names: Venus Flytrap, supported by Kew and IUCN; ハエトリグサ directly paired with Dionaea muscipula in [Kyoto Botanical Garden 2008](https://www.pref.kyoto.jp/plant/1212362554304.html) and [2010](https://www.pref.kyoto.jp/plant/1274172758158.html).

## Compact visual identity brief

- Individual/stage: one typical green-leaved mature rosette, no named cultivar; hero vegetative, card 3 flowering stage. No sex distinction. Red inner pigmentation varies and is not required on every leaf.
- Biological identity references: references/kyoto-plant.jpg ([2010 exact-taxon plant](https://www.pref.kyoto.jp/plant/images/o230521006.jpg)) for flat winged leaf stalks, single rosette attachment, paired clam-like lobes, edge cilia and open/closed leaves. references/kyoto-flowers.jpg ([2008 exact-taxon flower](https://www.pref.kyoto.jp/plant/images/o20052306.jpg)) for five white petals, green centre and flower stalk. These are cultivated geometry references only, not evidence of native substrate. Kyoto HTMLs retained. No Sarracenia photo included.
- Five defining features: (1) low basal rosette attached to moist soil; (2) a broad flat green leaf stalk ends in ONE two-lobed trap; (3) lobes share one central hinge/midrib and have fine marginal cilia, not thick animal teeth; (4) reddish inner surfaces with small sensory hairs on each lobe, distinct from marginal cilia; (5) a separate long leafless flower stalk rises from the rosette bearing white five-petalled flowers, not a bloom from a trap mouth.
- Growth form: small herb, traps centimetre-scale and much smaller than flowering stalk; rooted low on damp soil among grasses. Matte smooth leaf planes with fine veins and thin bristles. No woody branching trunk, floating leaves, giant jungle jaws or pitcher tubes.
- Natural pose: oblique low three-quarter view into a foreground open trap, with side-on/partly closed traps behind. Leaf origins and overlapping tips can be naturally hidden by the rosette/grass; do not force all leaves into view. No exact leaf number required.
- Trap mechanism is externally visible leaf movement, not normally hidden feeding anatomy. Insets show two intact lobes and the supporting flat stalk; Kyoto text supports typical three sensory hairs per lobe, never depict them as fangs or an internal tongue.
- False silhouettes: no Nepenthes pitcher, sundew sticky tentacles, monster face, eyes, lips or fleshy teeth; flower must be five-petalled rather than a daisy disc.
- Style reference only: generated_infographics/purple_frog_ja_2026-04-28.png, inspected before generation; controls broken pigment and warmth only.
- Card concepts: largest card 1 attached open-to-closed leaf study and small insect, arrow indicates motion without extra words; card 2 green trap leaf with sunlight and small arthropod to contrast energy/nutrients; tall card 3 complete small rooted rosette and long stalk with small white flowers. Exactly three visible numbered explanatory notes.
