# Japanese Direct Poster Prompt — Gerenuk

Create one complete vertical 2:3 Japanese educational infographic poster,
designed as a handmade naturalist field-notebook page specifically around the
Gerenuk's tall, upright browsing posture. The result must feel like one authored
composition, not a photograph with three equal software panels.

## Subject and scene

Show exactly one identifiable adult male Gerenuk, *Litocranius walleri*, rising
onto its hind legs to browse a thorny shrub in dry East African scrub. Keep the
whole animal visible and dominant: a slim tan antelope body, extremely long
neck and legs, small narrow head, very large ears, broad white eye rings, dark
preorbital marks, white lips and throat, darker brown saddle along the back,
pale belly, black knee tufts, black-tipped tail, and two ringed lyre-shaped
horns. Both hind feet are planted, both forelegs are raised, and all four limbs,
the complete neck, ears, horns, and tail are unobstructed and anatomically
attached.

Surround it with sparse acacia-like thorn scrub, warm ochre soil, dry grasses,
and distant low bush. Do not make a giraffe, dik-dik, impala, springbok, kudu,
goat, deer, or generic gazelle. No giraffe patches, branched antlers, spiral
kudu horns, extra animals, duplicated limbs, detached horns, fake maps, logos,
signatures, or watermarks.

## Layout and visual hierarchy

Use warm sand-colored fibrous paper, graphite contour drawing, muted watercolor,
and small accents of rust, olive, and charcoal. Let the upright body create a
tall open center. Integrate the title and scientific name at the top without
touching the ears or horns. Arrange exactly three irregular hand-drawn numbered
observation cards in the negative space around the silhouette, never across the
animal:

1. a small thorn-scrub horizon spot drawing for habitat;
2. a small head study showing the long neck, large ear, and white eye ring;
3. a small side-view browsing sketch with hind feet planted and leaves above.

Each card must contain one clearly visible number, one species-specific spot
illustration, and the full explanatory sentence. Use staggered card shapes and
sizes that follow the animal's tall outline; do not use three equal boxes. Put
the conservation footer quietly along the bottom edge. Keep all text fully
inside the canvas and highly legible at phone size.

## Text accuracy

Render the following Japanese text exactly. Do not translate, paraphrase,
shorten, respell, or add other words. Line wrapping is allowed only within an
item; preserve every character and the listed order. The space below the
scientific name is for habitat and the hero only: every observation sentence
may appear exactly once, inside its numbered card.

Text, verbatim:
"ジェレヌク"
"Litocranius walleri"
"東アフリカの 乾いたトゲやぶにすむ"
"長い首と脚 大きな耳と白い目の輪"
"後ろ脚で立ち 枝先の葉を食べる"
"IUCN Red List 2016：準絶滅危惧（NT）"

Use clear, correctly formed Japanese glyphs. There must be exactly three
numbered observation cards and no other labels, captions, badges, decorative
lettering, or repeated observation text.
