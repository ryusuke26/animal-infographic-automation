## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Erymnochelys madagascariensis* (Grandidier, 1867).
- English common name: Madagascan Big-headed Turtle. The 2008 assessment uses `Madagascar Big-headed Turtle`; both refer to the same accepted species.
- Supported Japanese name: マダガスカルヨコクビガメ.
- Editorial classification group: Reptiles.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Reptilia; Testudines; Pleurodira; Podocnemididae; Erymnochelyinae; *Erymnochelys*; *E. madagascariensis*.
- Broad native region: western Madagascar / Africa.
- Habitat: lowland west-flowing river systems, floodplain lakes, backwaters, and other slow freshwater habitats in western Madagascar.
- Naming caveats: historical combinations include *Dumerilia madagascariensis* and *Podocnemis madagascariensis*. Do not confuse this Malagasy podocnemidid with the Asian Big-headed Turtle *Platysternon megacephalum*.
- Global IUCN category: Critically Endangered (CR), criterion A4d, version 3.1.
- Assessment date and public year: assessed 15 January 2008; public footer year `2008`.
- IUCN check: the IUCN/TFTSG assessment reprint and current specialist citation confirm Global Critically Endangered (CR), criterion A4d, assessed 15 January 2008, under record `e.T8070A97396666`; checked 2026-08-09.
- Citation context: the current errata citation is `e.T8070A97396666`, while the DOI retains `T8070A12884059`; the errata context does not change the public assessment year.

## Visual identity guidance

- One adult freshwater turtle with a broad, slightly domed but streamlined dark-brown oval carapace; the rear shell edge is not a jagged snapping-turtle saw.
- Large broad head with a strong temporal casque, blunt snout, textured gray-brown skin, alert dark eye, and a long flexible neck; large does not mean grotesquely oversized.
- Four sturdy legs visibly attached at separate shoulder and hip origins, with clawed, webbed freshwater-turtle feet and clear negative space between near and far limbs.
- Stable natural pose: half-submerged beside a low branch or resting at a quiet river edge. One hero only; no forced neck-folding pose on the hero.
- Card 2 keeps head and neck visibly attached to the shell-front/body context. Card 3 shows one complete small turtle folding its neck sideways along and beneath the shell edge.
- Not the Asian Big-headed Turtle, snapping turtle, softshell turtle, tortoise, generic sea turtle, or crocodilian. No hooked snapping jaw, extra-long heavy tail, flippers, serrated shell, detached head, floating neck, or duplicated hero.

## Discovery doorway

In a slow western Madagascar river, a broad casque-covered head rises ahead of a dark oval shell. When the turtle folds in, its long neck takes a sideways route beneath the shell edge rather than withdrawing straight back.
