## Identity, names and scope
Accepted Draco volans Linnaeus, 1758, Common Flying Dragon / Common Gliding Lizard / Flying Lizard. Japanese public title ジャワトビトカゲ is used in Japanese reference literature; a Niigata University zoological article uses マレートビトカゲ for D. volans. Neither Japanese name alone settles the modern species limits: the scientific name and Java/Bali source scope control this package. Japanese title matches the named Japanese natural-history account below; no claim of a universally standardized name.
Lineage: Animalia > Chordata > Reptilia > Squamata > Agamidae > Draco > Draco volans.
Region: Indonesia, Java and Bali; pictured adult female and habitat from Petanu River, Bali secondary forest. Reptile Database retains broad legacy range text but explicitly separates former subspecies and excludes Singapore; the current IUCN and the exact-locality photographic account agree on Java/Bali. Do not transfer 1999 ADW Borneo/Philippines biology, D. volans sumatranus observations, or black/white spotted male membranes to the current species.

## Compact visual identity brief
- Individual/stage: adult female D. volans, Bali; no male body or orange banded male patagium as hero.
- Three reference roles: references/female-side.jpg (0680a) upright posture, folded body-side skin and blue throat; references/female-patagium.jpg (0676) diagonal full-body/limb/tail geometry, despite filename this is NOT a spread membrane reference; references/female-throat.jpg (0630) head/neck and small throat fold.
- Five defining features: (1) small angular pointed head with visible eye, photo0630 head; (2) narrow flattened tan-grey flecked scaled trunk/dark nape, photos0630/0676 dorsal region; (3) continuous very long tapering tail from pelvis, photo0676; (4) four normal lizard limbs with grasping digits and naturally folded lateral membrane, all three branch contacts; (5) small blue female throat fold below head, photo0680a throat and sex description. Real photos settle folded anatomy, not opened membrane skeleton.
- Natural pose: lengthwise on diagonal bark, head slightly raised; two near-side legs may be prominent and far legs hidden by branch/body. Do not force all limbs visible. Continuous tail and believable grips mandatory.
- Card1 is explicitly a qualitative attached rib/membrane anatomy study, supported by the genus diagnosis; not a claimed exact x-ray, rib-count reconstruction or photographic pose. The fan is lateral between limb bases; no hidden mouth anatomy is exposed.
- False silhouettes: fantasy dragon with shoulder wings; bat with finger-supported wing; D. sumatranus with black/white spotted male patagium. No unsupported ornament/crest.
- Uncertainty: photographs do not expose the spread female membrane; irregular markings are source-described. Keep the schematic simple; do not invent exact bone counts or internal organs.
