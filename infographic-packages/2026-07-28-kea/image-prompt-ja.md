# Japanese Direct Poster Prompt — Kea

Create one complete vertical 2:3 Japanese educational infographic poster,
designed as a handmade alpine naturalist's field-notebook page specifically
around the Kea's low olive silhouette and sudden orange underwing flash. The
result must feel like one authored composition, not a photograph with three
equal software panels.

## Subject and scene

Show exactly one identifiable adult Kea, *Nestor notabilis*, standing alert on
a lichen-speckled alpine rock in New Zealand's South Island. Keep the entire
bird visible and dominant: a large olive-green parrot with dark scalloped
feather edges, a long slender strongly decurved grey-black upper bill, dark
eye, grey cere and eye ring, dark legs, strong zygodactyl feet, and a narrow
dark tail. A mountain gust lifts one wing in a natural balancing motion,
revealing vivid orange-red underwing coverts; both wings, both feet, bill, and
tail remain anatomically attached and unobstructed.

Surround it with tussock, low subalpine scrub, cool grey rocks, a thread of
snowmelt water, and distant snowy Southern Alps. Do not make a kākā, kākāpō,
generic green parrot, macaw, parakeet, or lorikeet. No bright red bill, blue
head, owl-like facial disk, flightless body, rainbow plumage, extra birds,
duplicated wings or feet, detached feathers, fake maps, logos, signatures, or
watermarks.

## Layout and visual hierarchy

Use cool grey-blue fibrous paper, graphite and colored-pencil contour drawing,
muted watercolor, and small accents of alpine moss green, rust orange, and
snow white. Let the low rock and angled wing create a sweeping diagonal through
the center. Integrate the title and scientific name at the top without touching
the head or lifted wing. Arrange exactly three irregular hand-drawn numbered
observation cards in the negative space around the silhouette, never across the
bird:

1. a tiny forest-to-snowy-ridge transect drawing for habitat;
2. a small feather study contrasting olive outer plumage and orange underwing;
3. a small bill-and-foot study nudging and turning a twig.

Each card must contain one clearly visible number, one species-specific spot
illustration, and the full explanatory sentence. Shape the cards like torn
field-note scraps following the wing and rock contours; do not use three equal
boxes. Put the conservation footer quietly along the bottom edge. Keep all
text fully inside the canvas and highly legible at phone size.

## Text accuracy

Render the following Japanese text exactly. Do not translate, paraphrase,
shorten, respell, or add other words. Line wrapping is allowed only within an
item; preserve every character and the listed order. Every observation
sentence may appear exactly once, inside its numbered card.

Text, verbatim:
"ミヤマオウム"
"Nestor notabilis"
"南島の森から 高山まで暮らす"
"オリーブ色の羽 飛ぶと翼の下はオレンジ"
"くちばしと足で つつき、引き、確かめる"
"IUCN Red List 2017：絶滅危惧（EN）"

Use clear, correctly formed Japanese glyphs. There must be exactly three
numbered observation cards and no other labels, captions, badges, decorative
lettering, or repeated observation text.
