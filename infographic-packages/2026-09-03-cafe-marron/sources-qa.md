- Global familiarity check: English exposure is concentrated in Kew, IUCN, botanical-garden conservation, and rediscovery stories; exact Japanese general-audience exposure is sparse. It is not a recurring zoo, garden-centre, viral-video, or generic weird-plants staple.
- Discovery doorway: one plant begins with long narrow juvenile leaves, later carries shorter broad adult leaves, and opens greenish-yellow corollas that mature to white.
- Conservation doorway: the directly inspected current IUCN record confirms Global Critically Endangered (CR) under criterion D, assessed 29 June 2025, record `T33659A164117186`.
- Local-knowledge caution: Café marron is a locally named and iconic plant on Rodrigues. Public copy introduces it as unfamiliar to the reader and never claims that Rodriguan residents, students, forestry staff, or conservation teams do not know it.
- Viability: four official IUCN photographs and Kew's living-plant description agree on the opposite broad adult leaves, long narrow juvenile leaves, long-tubed five-lobed white flowers, and greenish-yellow-to-white maturation. The stage contrast supports three distinct illustrated cards without forcing incompatible growth stages onto the hero.

## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted scientific name: *Ramosmania rodriguesii* Tirveng.
- English public name: Café marron, used by Kew Plants of the World Online and Kew's species profile.
- Japanese public name: カフェマロン. Catalogue of Life records this Japanese vernacular through GBIF; no Japanese botanical standard-name list was confirmed, so the naming caveat remains here rather than on the poster.
- Editorial classification group: Plants.
- Exact lineage: Plantae; Tracheophyta; Magnoliopsida; Gentianales; Rubiaceae; *Ramosmania*.
- Broad native region: Rodrigues, Mascarene Islands, Mauritius / Africa and the western Indian Ocean.
- Habitat: subtropical or tropical dry to moist lowland forest, including heavily degraded former forest; the last truly wild plant grows in rich soil in a semi-exposed thicket at Mon Plaisir.
- Curiosity hook: its juvenile and adult foliage have sharply different shapes, while its corollas change from greenish yellow to white as they mature.

## Visual identity lock

- Reference role: the four official IUCN photographs are species-identity references only, never edit targets. They show broad glossy adult leaves, long narrow juvenile foliage, elongated tubular buds, and mature five-lobed white flowers.
- Hero: exactly one dominant flowering adult shrub or low tree in a natural Rodriguan forest-thicket setting.
