## Earlier retirement and cleanup

The earlier visual review over-weighted whether four limbs were simultaneously visible. The user correctly identified the more material failures: the face became a plug-like cone or pig-like disc instead of a skin-integrated horny nasal shield, the forefoot digit structure was wrong, and the two diagnostic digging claws on digits III and IV were either too small or became tusk-like. Fresh generation, localized face correction, and an English companion attempt all returned toward generic mole anatomy. The package was therefore retired at that stage rather than entering an open-ended generation loop.

Exactly four failed Japanese workspace PNGs were verified and moved to the Windows Recycle Bin on 2026-08-06. They remain recoverable until the Recycle Bin is emptied. The failed English generation was never copied into the package. The five original Image Gen cache files were also targeted, but the sandbox denied deletion; no stronger deletion method was attempted.

## Photo-informed sketch revisit

The user supplied five unverified web-image screenshots to clarify the shared *Notoryctes* anatomy. They were used only as temporary anatomy references and were not copied into the package. Visual Lock was corrected from a tiny wedge-shaped shield and furry short tail to one broad thick vertically oval nasal pad covering much of the conical nose, two long flattened dominant foreclaws on digits III and IV, and a short hard ringed tail ending in a horny knob. Because the two recognised *Notoryctes* species are difficult to separate morphologically, the screenshots are not treated as species-level evidence or publication assets.

One user-authorized Japanese sketch-style generation passed the direct-source gate at exact `1024x1536`. The hand-sketched medium, hard reduced tail, large digging claws, three numbered illustrated cards, and all six locked text lines were successful. The poster was rejected because the nasal pad became three stacked armour-like lobes instead of one continuous thick pad with at most one shallow crease, and the three subordinate digits were not resolved on the hero forefoot. The image was not copied into the package and no English companion was generated.
