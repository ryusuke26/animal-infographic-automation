## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Notoryctes typhlops* (Stirling, 1889).
- English common name for public copy: Southern Marsupial Mole.
- Official IUCN English names: Itjaritjari, Marsupial Mole, Southern Marsupial Mole.
- Current Mammal Diversity Database primary English name: Central Desert Marsupial Mole; Southern Marsupial Mole and Itjaritjari are listed aliases.
- Supported Japanese name: フクロモグラ.
- Native region and habitat: central Australian sandy deserts, especially dunes and adjacent swales with deep, loose sand.
- Lineage: order Notoryctemorphia; an Australian marsupial specialized for life beneath sand.
- IUCN check: the user-supplied official species-page screenshot and matching 11-page assessment PDF directly confirm Global Least Concern (LC), version 3.1, assessed 15 March 2014 and published 2016.
- Official record: `e.T14879A21965004`; DOI `10.2305/IUCN.UK.2016-1.RLTS.T14879A21965004.en`.
- Current population trend in the assessment: Stable. The assessment also says there is no robust population-size estimate, so no public count is used.
- Publication-safe status wording: `IUCN Red List 2014 : 低懸念 (LC)` / `IUCN Red List 2014: Least Concern (LC)`.

## Visual identity guidance

- One small pale-golden marsupial with a long tubular body and short dense silky fur. The tail is very short, hard and leathery, marked by rings, and ends in a small horny knob; it is not a furry mouse-like tail.
- Face: no visible eye opening and no external ear. The conical nose is covered at its tip by one broad, thick, vertically oval ochre-pink keratinized pad that occupies much of the visible nasal face. In front or three-quarter view it may show no more than one shallow transverse division, but it remains one continuous surface rather than stacked lobes; the nostril opening remains small. The pad is continuous with the face yet visibly substantial; it is not a tiny wedge, detached cap, paired pig-nostril disc, three-part armour plate, caterpillar-like stack, beak, gray cone, or fleshy placental-mole muzzle.
- Forefeet: five digits are present, but digits III and IV dominate as two long, greatly enlarged, flattened shovel-like digging claws. They may be gently curved and tan-to-ivory rather than uniform white triangles. The other three digits are much smaller and can be partly hidden at the claw bases; do not turn the animal into a two-toed form or draw five equal rodent-like fingers.
- Hind feet are flatter and smaller than the forefeet and push sand back. Do not enlarge every hind claw to match the two diagnostic foreclaws.
- Limb visibility is secondary to correct attachment, face, paw architecture, and scale. A far-side limb may be naturally occluded by the body or sand; never force an unnatural four-limb display.
- Show a shallow cutaway through soft red-gold dune sand with sparse spinifex above. A filled-in wake of disturbed sand follows behind the animal rather than a permanent hollow tunnel.
- Not a placental mole, golden mole, mole-rat, shrew, mouse, or larva; no large eyes, external ears, long tail, gray conical face plug, duplicated hero, or detached claws.

### User-supplied visual clarification

Five unverified web-image screenshots supplied on 2026-08-06 clarify the shared *Notoryctes* body plan across front, side, and feeding views. They are anatomy references only, not publication assets or proof that every pictured individual is *N. typhlops*. Australian Government guidance states that *N. typhlops* and *N. caurinus* are difficult to separate morphologically. Any Image Gen use must extract only the broad nasal-pad form, foreclaw architecture, tubular body, and hard reduced tail; it must not copy the photographs' compositions, backgrounds, prey, lighting, or surface poses.

### Accepted visual decision

On 2026-08-07 the user explicitly selected the source-gate-passing Japanese sketch poster after comparing its nasal-shield treatment with two localized edits. The visible pencil ridges are treated as stylized surface relief within one continuous broad pad, not as a public anatomical claim of separate armour plates. The selected poster clearly retains the broad face-integrated shield, two dominant foreclaws on each visible forefoot, no visible eye or external ear, and a hard ringed reduced tail. The English companion preserves the same established visual treatment.
