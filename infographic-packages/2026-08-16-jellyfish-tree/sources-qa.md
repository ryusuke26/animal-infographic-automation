### Jellyfish Tree / *Medusagyne oppositifolia* — selected

- Global familiarity check: English results are concentrated in botanic-garden, island-conservation, and specialist plant material, while Japanese searches do not show recurring general-audience coverage; it is not a household, zoo, aquarium, wildlife-video, or generic weird-organism staple.
- Discovery doorway: on exposed granite, a small tree produces an opened fruit whose many valves radiate from a central column like umbrella ribs.
- Conservation doorway: the official IUCN assessment directly confirms Global CR under C2a(ii), assessed 12 August 2025 as `e.T37781A262047825`.
- Local-knowledge caution: the tree belongs to Seychellois natural history and active local park and restoration work, so public copy presents it as an international viewer's discovery and never claims that nobody knows it.

### White-winged Nightjar / ハジロヨタカ / *Eleothreptus candicans* — not locked

- Local-knowledge caution: researchers and communities in Brazil, Paraguay, and Bolivia know the bird and its grassland sites; outside-facing copy must not call it universally unknown.

### Aldabra Flying-fox / アルダブラオオコウモリ / *Pteropus aldabrensis* — not locked

- English common name: Jellyfish Tree.
- Japanese display name: ジェリーフィッシュ・ツリー, a transparent transliteration of the documented English name; not claimed as an established standard Japanese name.
- Editorial classification group: Plants.
- Exact lineage: Plantae; Streptophyta; angiosperms; eudicots; order Malpighiales; family Ochnaceae; genus *Medusagyne*; species *M. oppositifolia*.

## Narrow Caution Run check

- Source conflict: a 2026 Kew public feature says the English name refers to jellyfish-like flowers, while Seychelles Parks and Gardens Authority says it comes from the fruit's shape. Botanical fruit descriptions independently show a many-parted capsule whose valves spread from a central column.
- Resolution: poster and X copy do not claim why the species was named. They only describe the directly supported opened-fruit geometry: valves radiating like umbrella ribs.
- Scope: no broader evidence expansion or user evidence request is needed.

## Visual identity guidance

- Show one complete small woody tree rooted in a shallow crack or pocket on an exposed rounded granite outcrop, not a generic rainforest canopy tree.
- Leaves are simple, dark green, thick/leathery, elliptic to oblong, with smooth margins, attached in clearly opposite pairs along slender twigs.
- A few small reproductive clusters may appear, but one opened fruit close-up must show many narrow woody valves radiating from a persistent central column like the ribs of an inside-out umbrella.
- The fruit remains a dry botanical capsule attached to a twig; it is not a jellyfish animal, flower tentacles, umbrella canopy, starburst blossom, sea anemone, or dangling seed pod.
- Card 1 shows the granite-outcrop habitat; card 2 shows one complete twig segment with paired opposite leaves; card 3 shows one attached opened fruit with its central column and radiating valves.
- No fake map, island flag, population counter, or invented flower color.

## Discovery doorway

On an exposed granite slope, a small tree carries thick paired leaves. After flowering, a many-parted fruit opens from one central column, turning a dry capsule into a delicate ring of umbrella-like ribs.
