Red fingers unfold from a pale "egg."
Devil's Fingers
*Clathrus archeri*

Native to Australia, this saprobic fungus lives on litter and woody debris. Carrion-like scent and red color attract flies that carry its spores: a strange little life guided by smell.

IUCN Red List: no public species assessment located (checked 2026)

## ALT text

A vertical field-notebook poster on warm cream paper, drawn in crayon and oil pastel. At center, Devil's Fingers emerges from a ruptured white egg-like casing among damp leaves and wood chips. Several separate coral-red arms carry dark olive-brown spore slime on their inner surfaces. Short notes explain that the arms unfold from an egg-like stage, the fungus decomposes litter and woody debris, and scent recruits flies to carry spores.
