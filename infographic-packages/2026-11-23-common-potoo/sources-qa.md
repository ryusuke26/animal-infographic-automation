## Evidence Lock

- Exact taxon and names: *Nyctibius griseus* (J.F. Gmelin, 1789) (IUCN, GBIF 2496811). English: Common Potoo. Japanese: ハイイロタチヨタカ (GBIF Japanese vernacular; タチヨタカ科). IUCN scope excludes the Northern Potoo (*N. jamaicensis*, Mexico–Honduras/Caribbean); native range Nicaragua and Costa Rica south to Argentina and Uruguay, plus Trinidad and Tobago.
- Lineage: Animalia > Chordata > Aves > Caprimulgiformes (IUCN; GBIF uses Nyctibiiformes) > Nyctibiidae > *Nyctibius*.
- IUCN check: https://www.iucnredlist.org/species/22689646/163600335 read directly 2026-11-23 — Global Least Concern ver 3.1, Date Assessed 04 December 2019, Year Published 2020, BirdLife International 2020, e.T22689646A163600335; trend Decreasing; habitat Forest, Savanna, Artificial/Terrestrial; upper elevation 1,800 m. Footer uses the assessment year 2019.
- Sources: (A) UWI Online Guide to the Animals of Trinidad and Tobago, *Nyctibius griseus* account (PDF; citing Skutch 1970, Borrero 1974, Cleere 1998, Hilty 2003, Voudouris 2015); (B) bioGraphic (California Academy of Sciences), "Assume the Position", 2019; (C) IUCN record above.
- Claim 1 (card 1): when disturbed the adult adopts a still stump-like posture, head raised, bill and neck outstretched, plumage compressed (A, Voudouris 2015); "pointing its beak skyward ... broken tree branch", rises slowly to a vertical position (B).
- Claim 2 (card 2): potoos have unusual slits in the eyelids which allow them to sense movement when the eyes are closed (A, citing Borrero 1974); "narrow folds in the upper eyelids enable the bird to peek out" (B). Caveat: Borrero's study covers the genus *Nyctibius*; both sources apply it to this species' account. Copy says "upper eyelids" and "sense movement", no count on the poster (figure asks for 2–3 small notches as a depiction, matching Wikipedia's "two or three slits").
- Claim 3 (card 3): no constructed nest; one egg laid in a depression/knot on a branch or broken stump; egg white with lilac markings; both sexes incubate (A: Skutch 1970, Sick 1997); single egg in a small depression along a branch (B).
- Main post (beyond cards): nocturnal; at dusk sallies from a perch to snatch flying insects such as moths; relatively large mouth (gape c. 26 mm, Haverschmidt) (A).
- Story reply (beyond cards): chick white, camouflaged to resemble a clump of fungus; once too large to hide under the parent it adopts the parents' freeze posture (A, Voudouris 2015); on moonlit nights a song of eight lamenting descending notes (A). Copy says "is said to", "note by note"; no count used.
- Not used: population numbers; incubation/nestling durations; independently moving eyelids; "tooth" on the upper mandible (single source).

  2. `Nyctibius_griseus_head.jpg` — public domain, Alejandro Bayer Tamayo (Quindío, Colombia): freeze posture, bill pointing straight up with tiny hooked tip, nearly closed eye with dark slit, streaked bark-like plumage.
  3. `Nyctibius_griseus-APA_de_Campinas-Vitorguimaraesc(001).jpg` — CC BY-SA 4.0, Vitorguimaraesc (Campinas, Brazil): night, relaxed, large yellow iris open, flat broad head, row of black breast spots, long banded tail hanging below the perch.
- Diagnostic invariants: (1) upright cylindrical body on a broken branch tip, outline continuous with the perch (ref 1; A, B); (2) freeze: bill pointed straight up, neck stretched, feathers compressed (ref 2; A); (3) tiny short bill with hooked tip, broad flat head, no bristles/ear tufts/facial disc (refs 2, 3; A "lacks bristles"); (4) very large eyes — yellow when open, slits when closed (refs 1–3; A); (5) bark-like mottled grey-brown/cinnamon/black plumage with a row of black breast spots and a long banded tail hanging down (refs 1, 3; A).
- Natural pose: upright perch, front three-quarter view; feet mostly hidden by plumage; gape closed.
- False silhouettes: owls (ear tufts, facial disc), Tawny Frogmouth (heavy broad bill, bristles), nightjars (lie flat along branch/ground), Great Potoo (larger, paler — not named in prompt to avoid priming).
