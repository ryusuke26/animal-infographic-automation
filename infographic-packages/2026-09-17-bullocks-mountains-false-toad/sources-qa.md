## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted scientific name: *Telmatobufo bullocki* Schmidt, 1952, accepted by the current IUCN record and Amphibian Species of the World.
- Taxonomic history and variants: it was formerly treated in the synonymy of *Telmatobufo venustus* before resurrection; *Telmatobufo bullockii* is an encountered incorrect spelling, not the accepted name.
- English public name: Bullock's Mountains False Toad, used by IUCN and Amphibian Species of the World. Bullock's False Toad is a shorter secondary English alias; Chilean sources use Sapo de Bullock and Rana montana de Nahuelbuta.
- Japanese public name: ブロックヤマモドキヒキガエル, a transparent package rendering of the English name. ブロッキーヘルメットガエル appears on one Japanese-language general site, but no standardized Japanese national-list name was confirmed.
- Editorial classification group: Amphibians.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Amphibia; Anura; Calyptocephalellidae; *Telmatobufo*.
- Broad native region: Nahuelbuta Range, Biobío and La Araucanía, south-central Chile / South America.
- Habitat: fast-flowing mountain streams and adjacent temperate native forest; adults use both aquatic breeding habitat and terrestrial forest.
- Curiosity hook: the larva is shaped for a torrent and the adult later walks away from that water into forest.

## Visual identity

- Individual/stage: one adult hero. Stage is material because Card 1 depicts the very different torrent tadpole and the juvenile reference shows a more contrasty yellow-black pattern than the olive-brown adult.
- Reference roles: `evidence/telmatobufo_bullocki_wikimedia_full_body.jpg` controls the adult's squat full-body silhouette, blunt head, prominent parotoid glands, smooth rounded dorsal gland masses, yellow interocular band and coherent four-limb attachment. All are identity references, never edit targets. `evidence/telmatobufo_bullocki_adult_female_reference.png` is a corroborating adult-stage view retained outside the initial three-image prompt bundle.
- Diagnostic features: (1) broad blunt head and robust toad-like body rather than a narrow tree-frog profile; (2) large dark eyes with a broad yellow band crossing the crown between them and pale yellow edging around the upper lip; (3) large oval parotoid glands directly behind the eyes plus many smooth rounded glandular masses across the olive-brown back; (4) long coherent limbs with broad webbed hind feet; (5) brown to olive-brown adult colour, never the juvenile's full yellow-black reticulation.
- Natural pose: low dorsal three-quarter adult on wet moss and leaf litter beside a cold forest stream. The near forelimb and hind limb are fully readable; the body may naturally occlude part of the far-side limbs. No limb is forced into view.
- False silhouette: not a generic warty bufonid (the back has large smooth rounded gland masses and a clear yellow interocular band); not a bright poison frog (robust olive-brown adult, no neon body); not a tree frog (no toe pads, no climbing pose); not *Telmatobufo venustus* (no black-violet body scattered with orange-red spots).
- Uncertainty: reference lighting varies and the juvenile is much more contrasty than the adult. Exact nodule count, unshown ventral pattern and fine toe-web boundaries are not turned into public claims.
