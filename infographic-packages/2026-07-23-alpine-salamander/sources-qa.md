# Sources and QA — Alpine Salamander

## Evidence Lock

- Accepted English common name: Alpine Salamander
- Accepted scientific name: *Salamandra atra* Laurenti, 1768
- Japanese public title: アルプスサンショウウオ
- Japanese naming basis: J-GLOBAL uses `アルプスサンショウウオ` for *Salamandra atra* in the indexed Japanese title of Guex & Chen (1986). `https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/public/200902002332370825`
- Naming caveat: Japanese sources also use `アルプスサラマンダー`. Public copy uses the J-GLOBAL form and keeps this variant note here.
- Taxonomy: Animalia; Chordata; Amphibia; Caudata/Urodela; Salamandridae; *Salamandra*. EEA/EUNIS records the accepted species and authority.
- Broad native region: Europe
- Native range: the Alps and Dinaric Alps of Europe. Public copy stays at the broad alpine-mountain level.
- Habitat: cool, damp mountain woodland, alpine meadow/pasture, dwarf-shrub heath, and other humid mountain ground; it is especially visible in wet weather.
- Locked behavior: a terrestrial, live-bearing salamander; embryos develop inside the female and the young are born fully developed for life on land, so reproduction does not require depositing eggs or larvae in open water.
- Exact visual identity: one small, slender, glossy black salamander in a safe walking posture; broad rounded head; small dark eyes; paired parotoid glands behind the head; four short limbs with four toes on each forefoot and five on each hindfoot; cylindrical body and round tapering tail.
- Color-form caveat: the widespread nominotypical form is uniformly black. A few localized Italian subspecies can show yellow or pale markings; the public poster depicts the widespread black form and does not claim every population is unmarked.
- Lookalike guard: not a yellow-spotted Fire Salamander, not a newt with a finned tail, not an axolotl with external gills, not a lizard with scales or claws, and not a generic black frog. Do not add yellow spots, dorsal fin, external gills, horns, spikes, scales, claws, or extra toes/limbs.
