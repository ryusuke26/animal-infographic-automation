## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted public scientific name: *Rhampholeon acuminatus* Mariaux & Tilbury, 2006, used by current IUCN, ITIS and The Reptile Database.
- Duplicate-screen variants: *Rhampholeon (Rhinodigitum) acuminatus*; English aliases Nguru Spiny Pygmy Chameleon and Nguru Pygmy Chameleon; documented misspelling *Rampholeon acuminatus*.
- English public name: Nguru Spiny Pygmy Chameleon, used by the current IUCN record and the BHS paper.
- Japanese public name: ヌグルコノハカメレオン, a published specialist rendering. アキュミナータスコノハカメレオン is another published rendering. Neither is presented as a formally standardized Japanese national-list name.
- Editorial classification group: Reptiles.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Reptilia; Squamata; Chamaeleonidae; *Rhampholeon*.
- Broad native region: Nguru Mountains, Morogoro Region, Tanzania / East Africa.
- Habitat: subtropical or tropical montane forest, especially leaf litter and low vegetation under canopy cover.
- Curiosity hook: the animal's Latin name means sharpened, matching the small sharp projections along its head and body and the adult male's broad nose process.

- Individual/stage: one adult male hero. Sex is material because the larger downward rostral extension is one locked card claim; the female appears only as a complete secondary comparison miniature in Card 2.
- Reference roles: `evidence/rhampholeon_acuminatus_zoovienna_full_body_2026-08.jpg` controls the robust leaf-like body, granular green-brown pattern, head points, grasping feet and tapered tail. `evidence/rhampholeon_acuminatus_zoovienna_rostral_2026-04.jpg` controls the slender adult-male profile, large downward-pointing rostral process, low helmet and continuous dorsal crest. Both exact-taxon images come from Tiergarten Schonbrunn's species page and are identity references, never edit targets.
- Diagnostic features: (1) a small laterally compressed leaf-like body rather than the tall body of a large chameleon; (2) a broad rounded rostral process that projects forward and downward in mature males, not one or more narrow horns; (3) many small pointed scales on the head and along a low continuous dorsal crest; (4) granular mottled olive, moss-green and brown skin with a paler underside; (5) four attached chameleon limbs with split grasping feet and one continuous tapered tail held straight or gently curved rather than tightly coiled.
- Natural pose: stable lateral three-quarter walk along one diagonal mossy twig. The near-side forefoot and hindfoot are readable; the body and twig may naturally occlude parts of the far-side limbs. The poster must reject merging, duplication or detachment without forcing every far-side endpoint into view.
- False silhouette: not a veiled or panther chameleon (no tall casque, bright bands or tightly curled long tail); not a horned chameleon (the male has one broad down-pointing rostral extension, no paired or needle horns); not a generic iguana or dragon (dorsal points remain small, feet grasp the twig in chameleon splits, body stays tiny and leaf-like).
- Uncertainty: the two official-zoo images document different adult proportions but do not label sex in their captions. The adult-male rostral difference is locked from Redbond et al. 2021 and its male/female Figure 1; fine spine counts and individual colour phases are not turned into claims.
