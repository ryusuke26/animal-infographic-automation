## Evidence route

The current official IUCN assessment accepts *Crax alberti* and records Global Critically Endangered under A4cd in 2025, record `e.T22678525A265023587`. BirdLife International's Red List team published the assessment basis, and BirdLife is the IUCN Red List Authority for birds. Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry supports the Japanese name アオコブホウカンチョウ. Smithsonian's National Zoo supports the northern Colombian lowland-forest habitat, adult male identity, and forest-floor feeding behavior.

Its glossy black body, curled crest, pale bill with a vivid blue cere and hanging blue wattle, white lower belly and tail tip, and pinkish legs remain complete and unobstructed. Three unequal illustrated cards follow the bird's upright silhouette: layered lowland forest, an attached head study of the blue cere and curled crest, and one complete small curassow feeding among fallen fruit and invertebrates.
