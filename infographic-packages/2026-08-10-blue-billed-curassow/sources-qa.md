## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Crax alberti* Fraser, 1852.
- English common name: Blue-billed Curassow. `Blue-knobbed Curassow` is a documented alternate English name; use the IUCN/Smithsonian primary form publicly.
- Supported Japanese name: アオコブホウカンチョウ.
- Editorial classification group: Birds.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Aves; Galliformes; Cracidae; Cracinae; *Crax*; *C. alberti*.
- Broad native region: northern Colombia / South America.
- Habitat: humid tropical lowland and foothill forest remnants in northern Colombia; the public claim uses the conservative phrase `northern Colombia's lowland forests`.
- Naming caveat: do not confuse *Crax alberti* with Yellow-knobbed Curassow *C. daubentoni*, Wattled Curassow *C. globulosa*, Great Curassow *C. rubra*, or a generic turkey.
- Global IUCN category: Critically Endangered (CR), criterion A4cd, version 3.1.
- Assessment/public year: 2025; public footer year `2025`.
- Assessment identifier: `e.T22678525A265023587`.
- Official assessment DOI: https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2025-2.RLTS.T22678525A265023587.en
- IUCN check: BirdLife International's Red List team concluded CR under A4cd in the 2025.2 reassessment process, and the finalized 2025 IUCN record uses the identifier above; checked 2026-08-10. BirdLife is the designated IUCN Red List Authority for birds.

## Visual identity guidance

- Show one adult male: a large upright ground bird with a coherent chicken-like galliform body, glossy black plumage, white lower belly and vent, white tail tip, curled black crest, pinkish legs, and a pale gray bill.
- The diagnostic blue structure is fleshy cere skin wrapping the base of the upper bill, with a small hanging blue wattle; it is not a fully blue hard bill, horn, helmet, or swollen balloon.
- Keep both legs visibly attached at separate hip origins with separate paths and feet; keep both folded wings attached to the torso and the tail centered at the pelvis.
- Stable natural pose: walking or pausing on humid forest leaf litter beneath lowland canopy light. One hero only.
- Card 2 keeps the head study attached to neck and upper-body context. Card 3 shows one complete small curassow feeding among fallen fruit and invertebrates, never an isolated bill, foot, or floating head.
- Not Yellow-knobbed, Wattled, Great, or Black Curassow; not a turkey, cassowary, guineafowl, peacock, or hornbill. No yellow bill knob, red or yellow wattle, casque, bare neck, fan tail, peacock train, enormous feet, duplicated bird, or invented anatomy.

## Discovery doorway

Leaf litter moves beneath a northern Colombian canopy. A tall black bird steps into a shaft of light, and the pale bill's fleshy blue base appears beneath a crown of tightly curled feathers.
