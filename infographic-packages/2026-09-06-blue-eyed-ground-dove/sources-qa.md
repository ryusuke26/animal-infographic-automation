## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted public scientific name: *Columbina cyanopis* (Pelzeln, 1870), used by the current IUCN record, BirdLife and Cornell Lab.
- Taxonomy caution: *Oxypelia cyanopis* and *Peristera cyanopis* are historical combinations; a 2025/2026 phylogenetic proposal recommends resurrecting *Oxypelia*, but current IUCN and BirdLife public records still use *Columbina cyanopis*. The package follows the current official public name and records the alternate combination for duplicate screening.
- English public name: Blue-eyed Ground Dove; Blue-eyed Ground-Dove is a supported orthographic variant.
- Japanese public name: アオメヒメバト, used by BirdLife International Tokyo's 2024 annual review and exposed through GBIF/Catalogue of Life. アオヒメバト is an alternate database form.
- Editorial classification group: Birds.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Aves; Columbiformes; Columbidae; *Columbina*.
- Broad native region: Botumirim, Minas Gerais, Brazil / Cerrado / South America.
- Habitat: terrestrial savanna and grassland; the rediscovered population uses pale white-sand Cerrado with low grasses and scrub.
- Curiosity hook: after about 75 years without confirmed documentation, an unfamiliar call led to a tiny russet dove with cobalt eyes and matching blue-violet wing marks.

## Visual identity

- Individual/stage: adult male, chosen because the cobalt iris, warm russet plumage and blue-violet wing spots are best expressed together. Female and juvenile are paler and are not mixed into the hero.
- Reference roles: `evidence/columbina_cyanopis_cornell_reference.jpg` is the real adult-male identity reference and controls silhouette, plumage zones, eye, wing marks, legs and tail. The IUCN page and BirdLife/SAVE sources control habitat and status. The reference photograph is not an edit target and its branch/background are not copied.
- Diagnostic features: (1) compact small ground-dove body with rounded head and short slim dark bill; (2) warm russet-orange crown, face, neck, breast and tail against olive-brown back/folded wing and pale belly; (3) vivid but small cobalt-blue iris with a subdued narrow bare-skin rim; (4) several separate iridescent blue-to-violet crescent/oval spots on the folded wing coverts; (5) two slim pink legs and one continuous rusty tapered tail.
- Natural pose: calm low-perch three-quarter side view in white-sand Cerrado, with one visible eye, one fully readable folded wing, both legs gripping the twig and the tail unobstructed. Far-side feather detail may be naturally hidden; the poster does not force an open-wing pose.
- False silhouette: not a Rock Dove or city pigeon (much smaller, compact ground-dove proportions and no grey barred wing); not a Ruddy Ground Dove (the target has a strongly blue iris and blue-violet covert spots plus a paler belly); not a Blue Ground Dove (the target male is russet/olive-brown, not broadly slate blue).
- Uncertainty: feeding and breeding details are poorly documented and excluded. The reference is only 640×480 but its major identity zones are clear; unreadable fine feather counts are not invented.
