## Evidence route

JAMSTEC's BISMaL accepts *Clione elegantissima* and records the Japanese name ハダカカメガイ and the current gastropod lineage. Aquarium Fukushima uses the same scientific and Japanese names for animals collected off Shiretoko, and supports the cold-water habitat, shell-less adult form, paired wing-like feet, and feeding cones. Exact official IUCN searches under the accepted name, the historical name *Clione dalli*, broader *Clione limacina* usage, and the English common name found no global species assessment on 2026-08-08. Public copy therefore uses the conservative completed-search wording and does not assign formal `IUCN NE`.

## Locked visual concept

One translucent sea angel swims diagonally upward beneath pale drift ice in cold blue water. Its paired wing-like parapodia remain separate and symmetrical, the tapered body shows warm pink-orange internal tissue, and two short head tentacles are visible. Three unequal observation cards follow the silhouette: an under-ice water vignette, a shell-less gastropod body study, and a gentle feeding diagram showing the complete small sea angel opening mouth cones toward one tiny shelled pteropod. The hero keeps its calm closed-head swimming posture.

## Visual acceptance

- Web image review of Toba Aquarium's 2018 and 2025 feeding photographs, the 1958 anatomical description, and the 1992 fast-strike study fixed the visual lock: the feeding center belongs at the top/front head apex, with three pairs of buccal cones projecting upward and outward while the head, neck, and body remain intact below.
- After several rejected anatomy or typography candidates, the user supplied a diagnostic sketch and two real feeding photographs. The sketch was used only for spatial topology and the photographs only for the cones' thickness, flexible curvature, uneven organic splay, compact origin, and relationship to the head.
- The user explicitly accepted the fresh Japanese poster. Its card 3 shows one complete small sea angel, exactly six thick translucent cones as three bilateral pairs, a compact head-top origin without a circular mouth collar or face cavity, and one small shelled pteropod above the cone tips.
- The English companion was generated from a fresh native 2:3 canvas using the accepted Japanese poster as a visual reference and the real-animal photographs as anatomy references. It preserves the same full-animal mechanism, countable six-cone arrangement, handmade medium, habitat, and three-card hierarchy.
- Neither accepted direct poster uses a rejected generated source as an edit target or image reference.
