## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Clione elegantissima* Dall, 1871.
- English common name: Sea Angel.
- Supported Japanese name: ハダカカメガイ（通称クリオネ）.
- Editorial classification group: Other invertebrates.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Mollusca; Gastropoda; Heterobranchia; Euopisthobranchia; Gymnosomata; Clionoidea; Clionidae; *Clione*; *C. elegantissima*.
- Native region and habitat: cold pelagic waters of the North Pacific, including the southern Sea of Okhotsk. Adults are recorded in winter and early spring cold-water masses, and Aquarium Fukushima records animals off Shiretoko during the drift-ice season.
- Naming caveat: older databases and literature may place North Pacific animals under *Clione limacina*. Current 2026 WoRMS/MolluscaBase, JAMSTEC BISMaL, Aquarium Fukushima, and recent peer-reviewed work recognize *C. elegantissima*; *Clione dalli* is listed by WoRMS as an unaccepted historical name for *C. elegantissima*.
- IUCN check: exact official searches under *Clione elegantissima*, *Clione dalli*, *Clione limacina*, *Clione okhotensis*, and Sea Angel did not surface a global species assessment on 2026-08-08.
- Publication-safe status wording: `IUCN世界評価は確認できず（2026年確認）` / `No global IUCN assessment confirmed (checked 2026)`. Public copy does not present `IUCN NE` as a formal category.

## Visual identity guidance

- One small translucent pelagic gastropod, vertically elongated and tapered at the tail, with warm pink-orange internal tissue visible through the body.
- Exactly two broad wing-like parapodia, one from each side, with separate body origins, separate outlines, and clear negative space.
- Two short head tentacles. Keep the calm hero's head closed and recognizable rather than split open.
- The feeding card must show one complete small sea angel upright with its head at the top, from a slightly elevated anterior three-quarter view that clearly exposes the head apex between the two short head tentacles.
- The mouth is a short dorsoventral/vertical slit on the anterior margin of the head. In the upright animal this is the very top/front apex, not a circular cavity across the face. The lips/skin folds retract laterally so that this small apical slit gapes; the intact head, face, neck, and body remain closed below it.
- Exactly six thick, translucent, flexible buccal cones emerge forward and upward from the compact head-apex feeding center as three bilateral pairs, three on each side. Their bends and angles remain organically unequal while all six stay separately countable around one small spiral-shelled pteropod.
- Visual inference from the anatomical descriptions and aquarium photographs: depict the cones as coming out of the top/front opening between the tentacles. Do not split the whole face vertically, do not open a large round cavity down the front of the head, and do not place the opening at cheek, neck, chest, or mid-body level.
- The six cones are not a beak, three tubes, squid arms, teeth, arrows, or detached floating parts.
- Cold blue under-ice water with suspended particles and soft shafts of light; no tropical reef, seafloor pose, jellyfish bell, fish fins, angel wings, halo, face, arms, legs, or feathers.

## Discovery doorway

Beneath winter drift ice, a shell-less snail seems to fly. Its soft paired feet beat like wings until a tiny shelled pteropod drifts close and the quiet swimmer reveals a much stranger feeding tool.

## Publication cautions

- Do not call the species formally `IUCN NE`; use the completed-search wording.
- Do not generalize the Shiretoko seasonal observation to every population or the whole year.
- Do not claim the feeding cones are always visible or that the animal eats only one prey species.
- Do not use *Clione limacina* as the public scientific name for the selected North Pacific identity.
- Do not accept a feeding inset unless the compact origin is confined to the head apex, all three cone pairs are countable, and the head, neck, and body remain intact below. Reject a circular mouth collar, cup-shaped cavity, lateral origin, or split extending below the apex.
