## Locked scope

- Accepted name: *Hogna ingens* (Blackwall, 1857); *Lycosa ingens* is the retrieval synonym used by the formal IUCN assessment.
- English public name: Desertas Wolf Spider, used by IUCN and EAZA.
- Japanese public name: デゼルタ・ウルフスパイダー, a transparent rendering of the official English name; no established standard Japanese common name was confirmed.
- Global status: IUCN Red List Critically Endangered (CR) under B1ab(ii,iii,v)+2ab(ii,iii,v), assessed 7 April 2014 and published in 2014.
- Public claims: it shelters beneath volcanic rocks in Castanheira Valley, adults are stocky grey spiders with black-and-white-banded legs, and they hunt invertebrates and can catch juvenile Madeira wall lizards.
- Discovery doorway: beneath one volcanic rock, a large wolf spider waits in the top-predator role usually imagined for much larger animals.

## Production result

- The first Japanese source passed the exact-2:3/full-canvas gate and rendered a strong eight-legged hero, but placed all three observation labels beneath the scientific name and left the cards without copy. Because this was a multi-region composition defect, it was rejected without becoming an edit target or reference image, then removed during the user-requested GitHub closeout.
- One allowed fresh-canvas Japanese retry used only the three authoritative references and the locked art direction in words. It passed with one complete adult female, eight separate walking legs, two distinct pedipalps, exact Copy Lock, volcanic under-rock habitat, and exactly three numbered illustrated cards with their own labels.
- The first English companion used the accepted Japanese poster plus the three authoritative references as reference images only. It preserved the species, habitat, palette, handmade medium, hierarchy, and card concept on a fresh complete canvas and passed without retry.
- Both direct and posting PNGs are `1024x1536` and pixel-identical within language.
