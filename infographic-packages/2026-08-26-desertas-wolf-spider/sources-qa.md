## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted scientific name: *Hogna ingens* (Blackwall, 1857). The 2022 integrative revision and current World Spider Catalog retain this combination.
- Synonym relevant to retrieval: *Lycosa ingens* Blackwall, 1857.
- English public name: Desertas Wolf Spider, used in the formal IUCN assessment and EAZA guidance. Deserta Grande Wolf Spider is a secondary English variant.
- Japanese public name: デゼルタ・ウルフスパイダー. No established standard Japanese common name was confirmed; this is a transparent katakana rendering of the official English name, not a claim of standardization.
- Editorial classification group: Other invertebrates.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Arthropoda; Arachnida; Araneae; Lycosidae; *Hogna*; *Hogna ingens*.
- Broad native region: Deserta Grande, Madeira archipelago, Portugal / Europe.
- Habitat: Castanheira Valley, a semi-arid volcanic valley with bare soil, native grasses, rocks, and crevices; adults shelter mainly under rocks and in shallow burrows.
- Curiosity hook: under one volcanic rock, an unusually large wolf spider waits in the top-predator role usually imagined for much larger animals.

## Visual identity lock

- One dominant complete adult female in a stable low pause at the mouth of a shallow silk-lined burrow beneath a dark volcanic slab. The spider must read as a stocky wolf spider, with a relatively large body for its leg span, not as a fuzzy tarantula, thin house spider, orb-weaver, black widow, or scorpion.
- Adult body is uniformly mid-grey; the cephalothorax is slightly darker with pale edging and a small pale patch near the abdomen joint. Chelicerae are black with subtle tiny red dots. Legs are robust and distinctly banded black and white.
- Exactly eight walking legs: each has a visible body origin, continuous path, separate endpoint, and negative space from its paired near/far leg. Two shorter pedipalps remain visibly separate and are not counted as extra legs. No fused, detached, floating, duplicated, hidden, or mirrored appendages.
- Habitat is semi-arid Castanheira Valley: steep ochre-and-charcoal volcanic slopes, dry streambed, sparse grasses, bare soil, angular basalt, and a cooler shaded under-rock refuge. No rainforest, beach, dune desert, deep cave, terrarium, or erupting volcano.
- Card 1 spot art: one small complete eight-legged spider beneath a volcanic rock in the valley, no fake map.
- Card 2 spot art: one complete adult from above showing the grey body and black-and-white leg bands, never detached-leg swatches.
- Card 3 spot art: one small complete spider and one complete juvenile Madeira wall lizard in a restrained natural predation scene, no gore or isolated body parts.
- Closest false silhouettes to avoid: generic shaggy tarantula; thin long-legged house spider; round orb-weaver abdomen; glossy black widow; all-brown wolf spider without white banding; threatening frontal spider with raised legs; any specimen with other than eight walking legs.
