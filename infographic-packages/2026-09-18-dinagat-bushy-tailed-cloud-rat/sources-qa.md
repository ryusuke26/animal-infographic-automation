  - `crateromys_australis_holotype_reference.png`: biological identity reference only; controls top-view body-to-tail proportions and confirms the sharply bicoloured tail. It is a preserved specimen, not a pose, habitat or lighting reference.
  - `generated_infographics/purple_frog_ja_2026-04-28.png`: style reference only; controls visible crayon/pastel stroke, warm paper and handmade editorial warmth, not anatomy, text or layout.
- Diagnostic features:
  1. The tail is about as long as the head-and-body and remains visibly hairy but more slender than in other *Crateromys*; it carries a conspicuous dark-and-white division rather than a uniformly coloured squirrel tail.
  2. The living body is grey-brown-reddish dorsally with a clearly lighter underside; no black-and-white body patches.
  3. The head has a tapered murid muzzle, long whiskers, small rounded dark ears and dark eyes; no squirrel-like crested tail or primate face.
  4. The body is a compact, large arboreal murid rather than a tiny mouse or an oversized porcupine-bodied rodent.
- Natural pose: lateral-dorsal three-quarter view, moving slowly along a diagonal branch after sunset. Near-side forefoot and hind foot grip the branch; far-side feet may be partly hidden by the body or foliage. Tail remains attached at the rump and extends along/behind the branch so the black-white division is readable.
- False silhouettes: not the smaller, fast, uniformly coloured Philippine Tree Squirrel; not another *Crateromys* with a uniformly luxuriant bushy tail; not a black-and-white patched giant cloud rat or common rat with a naked tail.
- Uncertainty: the field photographs are dark and partly occluded. Do not invent exact facial stripes, body spots, sex traits or a precise number of visible toes. The holotype colour is not assumed to represent every living individual.
