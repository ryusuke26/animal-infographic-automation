## Candidate slate

- Queen Alexandra's Birdwing / アレキサンドラトリバネアゲハ / *Ornithoptera alexandrae* — selected. Familiarity and discovery: it is known as the world's largest butterfly, but the record belongs to the broad brown-and-cream female while the smaller male is blue-green and black. Contribution to variety: a rainforest butterfly and pronounced adult sexual dimorphism contrast with the recent bird, two mammals, frog and fish; Insects are absent from the latest eight. Conservation context: current official IUCN Global Endangered (EN) under B2ab(iii), assessed 24 January 2018. Local-knowledge caution: the copy presents a viewer discovery and does not erase Papua New Guinean knowledge.
- Venus Flytrap / ハエトリグサ / *Dionaea muscipula* — viable familiar-species option. Familiarity and discovery: a familiar trap plant whose modified leaf closes through a touch-trigger mechanism. Contribution to variety: a low carnivorous rosette in Carolina wet pine habitat differs strongly from recent animal heroes and the recent cycad. Conservation context: IUCN Global VU under an assessment dated 30 June 2000 and marked Needs Updating; Kew confirms the accepted name and status. Rejected because the assessment is much older and the selected butterfly offered a current directly readable record plus a stronger bilingual visual contrast.
- Little Barrier Giant Wētā / *Deinacrida heteracantha* — strong nocturnal wingless-insect doorway and a body form unlike the recent weevil. Conservation context: global IUCN material is old and available summaries conflict with New Zealand's current national framing. Rejected because the jurisdiction and assessment-year boundary was not clean enough for a normal Quality Run.
- Ribbed Cachrys Oyster Mushroom / *Pleurotus nebrodensis* — a contrasting Mediterranean fungus tied to *Prangos* roots. Rejected because the earlier documented Japanese-name and cultivated-lineage identity problem remains unresolved; it would risk conflating a wild taxon with disputed cultivated material.
