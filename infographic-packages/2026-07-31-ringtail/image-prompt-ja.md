# Japanese Direct Poster Prompt — Ringtail

Create one complete true vertical 2:3 Japanese educational infographic poster,
designed as a handmade moonlit-canyon naturalist's field-notebook page around
the Ringtail's long striped tail, night-active face, and rocky habitat. The canyon, hero,
title, three observation cards, and footer must form one authored composition,
not three equal software panels.

## Subject and scene

Show exactly one identifiable adult Ringtail, *Bassariscus astutus*, in a
believable natural quadrupedal side or three-quarter view, walking cautiously
across a broad, nearly horizontal sandstone ledge after dusk. Show a slender
tan-grey body, pointed fox-like muzzle, very large dark eyes inside pale eye
rings, large rounded ears, short legs, and one long bushy tail with crisp
alternating black and pale rings. The body weight and paw placement must look
physically plausible, with all four limbs visibly connected from torso to paw.

Trace exactly four continuous limbs from torso to paw. Do not make a domestic
cat, civet, genet, lemur, raccoon, coati, fox, ferret, skunk, red panda, or
Central American Cacomistle. No cat paws, striped torso, raccoon face mask,
prehensile tail, extra limbs, detached paws, broken joints, duplicated tail,
hidden limb attachment, extra hero animals, humans, fake map, logo, signature,
or watermark.

## Layout and visual hierarchy

Use warm fibrous paper, graphite, colored pencil, muted gouache, and a palette
of sandstone ochre, pinyon green, moonlit blue-violet, charcoal, and tail-ring
cream. Let the curved ringed tail lead the eye from the title through the hero.
Keep the area immediately below the scientific name blank. Place exactly three
unequal irregular numbered observation cards in the negative spaces around the
walking silhouette without covering the face, tail, or any limb:

1. a small canyon-and-water habitat cutaway with pinyon-juniper scrub;
2. a close study of the large eye, rounded ear, and black-and-pale tail rings;
3. one small but complete Ringtail silhouette descending headfirst on a rock
   face, with torso, head, all four attached limbs, paws, and one attached
   ringed tail; use subtle arrows near the hind ankles to show the 180-degree
   turn.

Each card must have one clear number, one species-specific spot illustration,
and its full explanatory copy. No three equal boxes. Keep the hero dominant
and unobstructed. Integrate the quiet footer at the bottom. Make every line
legible on a phone.

## Text accuracy

Render only the following Japanese text exactly. Do not translate,
paraphrase, shorten, respell, repeat, or add words. Line wrapping is allowed
inside an item; preserve every character and order. Each observation appears
exactly once inside its numbered card.

Text, verbatim:
"カコミスル"
"Bassariscus astutus"
"岩場と水辺 米国南西部からメキシコに暮らす"
"大きな目、しま尾 夜に動くアライグマの仲間"
"後ろ足を180度回す 岩や木を頭から下りる"
"IUCN Red List 2015：低懸念（LC）"

Use clear correctly formed Japanese glyphs. Exactly three numbered illustrated
cards; no extra labels, captions, badges, decorative text, logos, or
watermarks.
