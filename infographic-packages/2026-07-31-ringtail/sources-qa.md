- English common name: Ringtail
- Japanese common name: カコミスル
- Broad native region: North America
- Lineage: raccoon family, Procyonidae
- Native range: southwestern United States through Mexico
- Habitat: rocky outcrops, canyons, talus, scrub, woodland, and riparian areas
- Global IUCN category: Least Concern (LC)
- Scope: Global

- Publication year: 2016

## Locked Public Claims

1. Habitat: rocky canyons and water-associated habitats from the southwestern
   United States into Mexico.
2. Visible identity: large dark eyes and a very long black-and-white ringed
   tail on a small raccoon relative active mainly at night.
3. Behavior: the hind feet can rotate 180 degrees so pads and claws keep
   contact during a headfirst descent.

Public copy omits population numbers, population trend, local legal status,
threat ranking, blame, rescue framing, and unsupported superlatives.

## Visual Identity Guidance

- Show exactly one adult Ringtail in a believable natural quadrupedal side or
  three-quarter view, walking across a broad, nearly horizontal sandstone
  ledge after dusk.
- Slender tan-grey body, pointed fox-like muzzle, very large dark eyes with
  pale eye rings, large rounded ears, short legs, and a long bushy tail with
  crisp alternating black and pale rings.
- Trace all four hero limbs from torso to paw with plausible weight and joint
  placement. Explain the headfirst descent in card 3 with one complete small
  Ringtail whose four limbs and tail remain attached.
- Use a rocky canyon with a nearby ribbon of water, pinyon-juniper or scrub,
  and moonlit blue-violet shadows.

## Negative Constraints

- Not a domestic cat, civet, genet, lemur, raccoon, coati, fox, ferret, skunk,
  red panda, or Central American Cacomistle.
- No cat paws, striped torso, raccoon face mask, prehensile tail, pointed lemur
  muzzle, oversized predator teeth, extra limbs, detached paws, duplicated
  tail, hidden hind-foot attachment, extra animals, fake map, humans, bait,
  logo, signature, or watermark.
