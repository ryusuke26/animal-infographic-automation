## Visual identity brief

- Individual/stage: one adult as the dominant hero. Use a robust adult with natural wear; do not exaggerate sexual size difference or claim a sex.
- Reference roles:
  - `references/sanbi_suricata10_geometric_tortoise_habitat.png`: biological identity reference only for adult three-quarter proportions, high domed shell, continuous yellow-on-dark network, mottled head and limbs, low succulent/restio habitat and broken shade.
  - `references/sanbi_suricata01_geometric_tortoise_pattern.png`: biological identity reference only for a second exact-taxon adult and the way rays align across multiple scutes.
  - `generated_infographics/purple_frog_ja_2026-04-28.png`: style reference only for broken crayon/oil-pastel pigment, exposed warm paper and handmade editorial warmth; never tortoise anatomy, copy or layout.
- Diagnostic features:
  1. Strongly domed oval terrestrial shell, small enough to sit low among shrubs; not a flattened freshwater turtle.
  2. Dark black-brown dorsal scutes each carry several golden-yellow rays from a small center; rays meet across neighbouring scute seams and read as one geometric network.
  3. Marginal scutes are relatively tall; shell edge is smooth rather than serrated or flared, and there are no extreme tent-like pyramids.
  4. Head and limbs are yellow-olive to tan with irregular dark spots; forelegs have unequal protective scales, front feet five toes and hind feet four where visible.
  5. Habitat is low renosterveld/fynbos shrubland with restios, herbs, small succulents and broken shadows on sandy-gravel soil.
- Natural pose: low three-quarter side view of one adult walking between short shrubs. Near forefoot and hind foot may be visible while the far rear leg or individual toes are naturally occluded. The top half of the shell remains unobstructed for pattern tracing.
- False silhouettes: not Indian star tortoise with thicker high-contrast spoke bursts and a different body range; not radiated tortoise with a flatter wider yellow-ray system; not tent tortoise with exaggerated pyramidal scutes; not a leopard tortoise with yellow blotches rather than linked rays.
- Uncertainty: pattern thickness, wear and yellow tone vary naturally. The prompt locks network logic, not an identical count of rays per scute. Natural limb overlap is accepted; extra limbs, fused feet or impossible toe attachment are not.
