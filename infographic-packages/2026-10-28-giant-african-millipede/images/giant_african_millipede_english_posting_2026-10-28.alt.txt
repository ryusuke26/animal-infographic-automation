Vertical cut-paper collage on deep forest teal, with rust and ochre leaf litter. One charcoal cylindrical Giant African Millipede walks in a loose curve, showing many trunk rings, short brown legs beneath, a small head and short antennae. Three numbered illustrated studies explain two pairs of legs on most double trunk rings, feeding on decaying plants, and coiling with hard shell outward. Studies show an attached leg detail, head at a fallen leaf, and a protective spiral. The top names Archispirostreptus gigas; the footer gives the global IUCN Least Concern assessment from 2017.
