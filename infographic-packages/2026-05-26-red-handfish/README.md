## Status Note

The Tasmanian listing statement was prepared in 2019 and published in 2020, and still displays `IUCN Red List: Critically Endangered (pending)`. The subsequent published IUCN assessment is `Stuart-Smith, Edgar & Last 2020, e.T123423510A123424379`, shown as `Critically Endangered` in Fishes of Australia and Reef Life Survey. Public copy therefore uses the completed 2020 IUCN assessment and keeps Tasmanian and Australian legal statuses separate.

## Image Identity Guidance

Show a single Red Handfish on a shallow temperate reef seafloor in south-eastern Tasmania. Essential cues: a tiny squat anglerfish-like fish, elongated body tapering to the tail, prominent modified pectoral fins shaped like little hands resting on sand or rock, red to pink-red mottled colour with some pale/blue-white fin edging, and low green seaweed or seagrass nearby. It should be walking on the bottom, not free-swimming. Avoid a human hand, a frog, an octopus, a generic tropical reef fish, a seahorse, giant scale, fake maps, or a population-count graphic.
