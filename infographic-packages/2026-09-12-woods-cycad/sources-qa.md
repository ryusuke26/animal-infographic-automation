- Global familiarity check: English exposure is concentrated in botanic gardens, cycad circles and occasional rare-plant features; Japanese exact-name exposure is sparse and chiefly horticultural. It is not a recurring zoo, aquarium, mainstream wildlife or generic weird-animal staple.
- Discovery doorway: every known cultivated plant is a male clone descended from the single original, yet each can raise a crown of vivid orange pollen cones.
- Conservation doorway: the directly inspected current IUCN record `T41881A243434007` lists Global Extinct in the Wild (EW), assessed 20 May 2020.
- Local-knowledge caution: public copy names oNgoye Forest, KwaZulu-Natal and botanical-garden cultivation rather than calling the species unknown to South Africans or cycad specialists.
- Visual viability: three role-distinct exact-taxon Kew photographs settle the stout leaf-base-covered trunk, radial glossy pinnate crown and clustered orange pollen cones.

## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted public scientific name: *Encephalartos woodii* Sander, accepted by Kew Plants of the World Online.
- Duplicate-name coverage: heterotypic synonym *Encephalartos altensteinii* var. *bispinosus* J.M.Wood; English Broodboom and Wood-se-broodboom; Wood's Cycad and Woods Cycad.
- English public name: Wood's Cycad, supported by IUCN, Kew and SANBI.
- Japanese public name: ウッドオニソテツ, documented by Satake's Japanese cycad collection page; used as a public horticultural name without claiming national standardization. The same page warns that its historical count and biological notes may be outdated, so it supports the display name only.
- Editorial classification group: Plants.
- Exact lineage: Plantae; Tracheophyta; Cycadopsida; Cycadales; Zamiaceae; *Encephalartos*.
- Broad native region: oNgoye Forest, KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa / Africa.
- Habitat: forest edge on a steep south-facing slope in the species' former wild range; the living lineage is now maintained in cultivation.
- Curiosity hook: what looks like a grove is the branching continuation of one male plant.
