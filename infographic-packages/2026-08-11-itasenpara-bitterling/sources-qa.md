## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Acheilognathus longipinnis* Regan, 1905.
- English public name: Itasenpara Bitterling. The official IUCN assessment lists both `Deepbodied Bitterling` and `Itasenpara Bitterling`; FishBase uses `Deepbody bitterling`. Use `Itasenpara Bitterling` publicly to preserve the Japanese name without implying that it is the only supported English common name.
- Supported Japanese name: イタセンパラ.
- Editorial classification group: Fishes.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Teleostei; Cypriniformes; Acheilognathidae; *Acheilognathus*; *A. longipinnis*. Older Japanese government pages place bitterlings within Cyprinidae; public copy does not expose the family-level convention.
- Broad native region: central and southern Japan / Asia.
- Habitat: quiet floodplain waters, including river backwaters, channels, small rivers, and lakes in the Nobi Plain, northwestern Toyama Plain, and Lake Biwa-Yodo system.
- Global IUCN category: Endangered (EN), criterion B2ab(ii,iii,v), version 3.1.
- Assessment date/year: 7 December 2017; public footer year `2017`.
- Publication year: 2019; this is not substituted for the public assessment year.
- Official assessment record: `e.T213A116034178`; DOI `10.2305/IUCN.UK.2019-2.RLTS.T213A116034178.en`.
- IUCN check: the directly inspected 10-page official assessment PDF and matching official species-page capture agree on the accepted scientific name, Global scope, EN category, criterion B2ab(ii,iii,v), and assessment date 7 December 2017. The PDF separately records publication in 2019 and the assessment identifier `e.T213A116034178`.
- Current-status cross-check: FishBase version 2025-1 also lists EN and the 2017 assessment date; IUCN Red List version 2019-2 Table 7 records *A. longipinnis* as moving from VU to EN.
- Naming and taxonomy sources: Japan Ministry of the Environment; National Museum of Nature and Science; Eschmeyer's Catalog of Fishes.
- Naming caveat: do not confuse this species with generic bitterlings, Rosy Bitterling, Rhodeus species, or aquarium barbs. The name is one word in Japanese: イタセンパラ.

## Visual identity guidance

- Show one adult breeding male in side view: a small Japanese bitterling with a very thin, laterally compressed but unusually deep body, high arched back, small pointed head, complete lateral line, no barbels, and large dorsal and anal fins. Large individuals can look broadly finned because the fin rays lengthen.
- Base coloration is silvery with olive-gray along the back; the breeding male's lower side and belly carry a restrained reddish-purple flush. Do not turn the whole fish neon magenta or bright tropical blue.
- Keep one eye, gill cover, pectoral fin, pelvic fin, dorsal fin, long anal fin, and caudal fin anatomically attached and readable. Do not add a barb, whiskers, long ornamental streamers, cichlid lips, or a goldfish tail.
- Habitat is a quiet shallow floodplain backwater or connected channel with submerged grasses, soft silt, diffuse green-brown water, and a unionid freshwater mussel partly settled on the bottom. No coral, reef, aquarium glass, or generic tropical river.
- Card 3 may show one complete small female beside one intact mussel and a restrained scientific cutaway of the mussel cavity. Keep the fish complete; do not show a detached ovipositor or floating reproductive organ. Eggs are inside the mussel, never glued to the outer shell.
- One hero only. No duplicated hero, school of lookalike barbs, carp, goldfish, tropical tetra, or shellfish anatomy invented for drama.

## Discovery doorway

In a quiet autumn backwater, a paper-thin silver fish deepens to reddish purple. Beside it lies a freshwater mussel that will shelter the fish's early life until spring.
