## Evidence Lock Summary

- Accepted English common name: American paddlefish. FishBase displays Mississippi paddlefish as a common name; English public copy uses American paddlefish for clarity.
- Japanese common name: アメリカヘラチョウザメ. This is a safe descriptive Japanese rendering for the American/Mississippi paddlefish and is used as the Japanese title.
- Accepted scientific name: *Polyodon spathula* (Walbaum, 1792).
- Taxonomy caveat: the original combination was *Squalus spathula* Walbaum, 1792; Eschmeyer's Catalog of Fishes lists it as currently valid as *Polyodon spathula* (Walbaum, 1792).
- Native range: North America; Mississippi River basin from southwestern New York to Montana and south to Louisiana, and Gulf Slope drainages from Mobile Bay, Alabama, to Galveston Bay, Texas. Former Great Lakes-basin occurrence is noted as likely extirpated by FishBase.
- Habitat: freshwater, slow-flowing large rivers, lakes, and impoundments; public copy narrows this to large, slow river water.
- Behavior and distinctive traits: long canoe-paddle-shaped snout/rostrum; feeds on plankton; rostrum/head sensory system is used to detect weak electrical signals in the water column; broad mouth and gill-raker filtering are important visual cues.
- Conservation status footer:
  - Japanese: `IUCN Red List 2019：危急（VU）`
  - English: `IUCN Red List 2019: Vulnerable (VU)`
- Assessment year and basis: user-supplied official IUCN Red List page and screenshot confirm Paddlefish / *Polyodon spathula*, Vulnerable (VU), criteria A2cd, Global scope, and Last assessed 14 September 2019. The visible citation line is Moore, M. & Rider, S. 2022, *Polyodon spathula*, The IUCN Red List of Threatened Species 2022: e.T17938A81763841. FishBase 2025-2 independently displays the same IUCN VU / A2cd / 14 September 2019 route.
- CITES/legal: FishBase displays CITES Appendix II. This is recorded as source context only and is not used in the poster footer.
- Threats: public copy does not mention specific threats beyond the quiet VU status label, so no public threat claim is included.

## Visual Identity Guidance

- Show one large American paddlefish in a murky but gentle big-river scene.
- Body: long smooth gray-blue freshwater fish, scaleless look, broad head, very wide mouth, small lateral eyes, large pointed operculum/gill-cover flap, forked heterocercal shark-like tail.
- Rostrum: very long, broad, flat canoe-paddle or spoon-shaped snout extending forward from the head; not a sword, saw, duck bill, platypus bill, shark nose, sturgeon barbels, or catfish whiskers.
- Feeding cue: open mouth with soft plankton dots and faint flow lines is acceptable if simple; keep one hero fish, no dense diagrams.
- Habitat cue: Mississippi-basin-style broad river water, muted green-brown current, submerged plankton specks, no ocean, reef, aquarium tank, fishing scene, caviar scene, fake map, or dam scene.
- Text layout: title and scientific name at top, exactly three numbered observation note cards with small icon/spot illustrations, quiet status footer at bottom.
