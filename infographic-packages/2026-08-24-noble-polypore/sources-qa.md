## Exactly three public claims

1. Habitat: it forms long-lived conks at the bases of very old large firs and old stumps in Pacific Northwest old-growth forest.
2. Visible identity: coarse white surface fibres darken with age and can appear green where unicellular algae live among them.
3. Life history: the perennial fruitbody adds pore-bearing layers, and more than 100 tube layers have been found in one fruitbody.

## Visual identity lock

- One dominant attached fruitbody at the root collar of one massive upright fir or old fir stump. It must visibly emerge from and wrap against the woody substrate; never float on soil and never stand on a central mushroom stem.
- Relative scale must be large and architectural: a broad, thick, low shelf or irregular semicircular conk spanning much of the trunk base, not a hand-sized bracket and not a cluster of ordinary cap-and-stem mushrooms.
- The upper surface is a coarse shaggy mat of fibrous white-to-cream growth, weathered cinnamon-brown zones, and natural algae/moss green patches with embedded needles and small debris. The thick pale scalloped growing margin remains visible.
- The underside is pale and pore-bearing, with stacked horizontal tube layers suggested in a cutaway or card; no gills, teeth, petals, roots, tentacles, coral branches, or starfish lobes.
- Growth-form variation is allowed within the documented sessile, imbricate, or centrally substipitate forms, but the hero uses the visually clearest low shelf attached to a trunk base. It must not become a flower, coral, animal hide, blanket lying loose on the ground, generic artist's conk, or giant rosette.
- Habitat must read as cool wet Pacific Northwest old-growth fir forest: massive dark bark, sword ferns, moss, wet needles, low mist, and subdued forest light. Not a tropical jungle, garden lawn, cut lumber display, indoor specimen table, or fantasy forest.
- Card 1 spot art: one small complete side-view conk visibly attached at the root collar of a very old fir, with tree-scale cue.
- Card 2 spot art: a close but still attached surface vignette showing coarse white fibres interwoven with algae-green patches; no detached swatch.
- Card 3 spot art: a clean cross-section icon of the attached conk showing many alternating pale and cinnamon tube layers, not gills or tree rings.
