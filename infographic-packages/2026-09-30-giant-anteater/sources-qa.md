- Accepted name and lineage: *Myrmecophaga tridactyla* Linnaeus, 1758; Animalia > Chordata > Mammalia > Pilosa > Vermilingua > Myrmecophagidae. [Mammal Diversity Database](https://www.mammaldiversity.org/taxon/1000442/) and formal IUCN assessment. Original combination is unchanged; English aliases include Ant Bear and Great Anteater.
- Public names: Giant Anteater; オオアリクイ, cross-checked with [Yokohama Zoological Gardens inventory](https://www.city.yokohama.lg.jp/kurashi/machizukuri-kankyo/midori-koen/zoo_garden/nenpou.files/0135_20251002.pdf).
- Habitat/range: Central and South American forest, savanna and grassland; [San Diego Zoo](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/giant-anteater). The scene uses South American open woodland/grassland without a false precise locality.
- Claim 1: large front claws curl into the forefeet, with walking on the outside of the fists to keep them sharp; [San Diego Zoo](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/giant-anteater).
- Claim 2: no teeth; a long sticky tongue gathers ants and termites; [San Diego Zoo](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/giant-anteater), [Smithsonian National Zoo](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/giant-anteater). No daily intake or tongue-speed numbers are used.
- Claim 3: bushy tail can serve as a cover while resting; [San Diego Zoo](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/giant-anteater).
- Discovery doorway: the unusual forefoot walk, then tongue and tail as connected life details. Conservation remains quiet context.

## Compact visual identity brief

- Individual/stage: one adult; no sex-specific feature asserted. No baby riding the back.
- Reference roles: [Mammal Diversity Database adult side-view photograph](https://www.mammaldiversity.org/taxon/1000442/) for overall walking silhouette and long tail; [San Diego Zoo exact-taxon photographs and account](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/giant-anteater) for long narrow snout, forefoot gait and bushy tail; [Smithsonian account](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/giant-anteater) for feeding behavior. The local purple-frog image is style reference only.
- Defining features: (1) small head ending in a very long, narrow tubular snout, with tiny mouth at its tip [Zoo head view]; (2) grey-brown shaggy torso with a bold black shoulder/chest wedge edged pale cream/white, rather than stripes covering the full body [MDD side view, Zoo photos]; (3) bulky forelimbs with enlarged curved claws tucked inward when walking [Zoo gait description/photos]; (4) slimmer hind legs and four coherent connected limbs, allowing far legs to overlap [MDD side view]; (5) very long, broad, shaggy tail continuous with rump [MDD side view, Zoo photos].
- Natural pose: broad side or three-quarter side walk at ground level, snout angled down; far front or hind foot may naturally hide behind the near side. The hero does not need to protrude its tongue while walking. Card 2 can show a connected snout-to-nest feeding study.
- False silhouettes: not a short-snouted tamandua with a prehensile narrow tail; not an aardvark with rabbitlike ears; not a pangolin with scales. No detached tongue, human hands, extra feet or tail rising as a separate animal.
- Uncertainty: coat tones vary among photographs; keep the black/cream shoulder wedge and shaggy brown-grey body rather than specifying exact shade. Do not prescribe exact toe count from an unreadable walking view.
