## Evidence Lock Summary

- Accepted English common names: Hine's Emerald; Hine's emerald dragonfly.
- Japanese title: `ハインズ・エメラルド`. No established Japanese standard common name was confirmed in the checked authoritative sources. The concise katakana rendering is used publicly; the naming caveat stays here instead of appearing as an editorial poster label.
- Accepted scientific name: *Somatochlora hineana* Williamson, 1931. No subspecies are recognized in the 2011 COSEWIC account.
- Native range: current authoritative records include Illinois, Michigan, Missouri, Wisconsin, and Ontario. Public copy uses the safe broad wording `北米` / `North America` and does not show a map.
- Habitat: groundwater-fed wetlands dominated by grass-like plants, usually with shallow slow flow; many occupied sites are associated with dolomitic bedrock or calcareous limestone and crayfish.
- Distinctive adult identity: brilliant green mature eyes; dark metallic-green thorax with two creamy-yellow lateral stripes on each side; long, slender blackish-brown abdomen; two pairs of clear wings and three pairs of legs.
- Larval behavior: larvae have been recovered from crayfish burrows during low-water periods and winter. The 2024/2025 status review identifies the associated crayfish over much of the range as *Lacunicambarus nebrascensis* and describes the burrows as refuges during habitat drying and overwintering.
- Conservation/status footer:
  - Japanese: `IUCN Red List 2018：低懸念（LC）`
  - English: `IUCN Red List 2018: Least Concern (LC)`
- IUCN check: confirmed. Official page `https://www.iucnredlist.org/species/20342/129856416` identifies Hine's Emerald / *Somatochlora hineana* as Least Concern (LC), Global scope, last assessed 8 June 2018, with the assessment published in 2019 as Abbott & Cashatt 2019, `e.T20342A129856416`. The live official page was read successfully in the in-app Browser on 13 July 2026.
- Population trend on the IUCN page: Stable. This is recorded for evidence context and is not used as a public poster claim.
- U.S. jurisdiction context: listed as Endangered under the U.S. Endangered Species Act on 26 January 1995. The final status review dated 16 December 2024 and published 17 January 2025 recommends `No change needed`. This U.S. legal category is retained in source/context replies, not substituted for the available global IUCN footer.
- Jurisdiction caveat: IUCN LC is a global Red List assessment; U.S. ESA Endangered is a U.S. federal legal category. They are not contradictory and must not be collapsed into one label.
- Threats: not used in public copy.
- Population numbers: none used.

## Visual Identity Guidance

- Show one adult Hine's emerald dragonfly in a groundwater-fed sedge fen with a narrow shallow streamlet, low grasses/sedges, and a soft limestone/dolomite cue.
- Body plan: one true dragonfly with a head, thorax, long slender abdomen, six legs, and exactly four clear membranous wings. Use a natural low patrol or perched posture.
- Diagnostic color: mature brilliant emerald-green compound eyes; dark metallic-green thorax; two distinct creamy-yellow lateral stripes visible on the near side; blackish-brown narrow abdomen.
- Habitat cues: sedges, cattail used sparingly, shallow slow sheet flow or streamlet, marl/limestone hint, nearby meadow and forest edge. No large open lake, fast river, tropical swamp, desert, aquarium, or fake map.
- Lookalike risks: other *Somatochlora* species can be extremely similar. Do not claim visible species-level certainty from terminal appendages in the illustration. Avoid damselfly posture, clubtail abdomen, broad butterfly wings, neon fantasy glow, extra legs/wings, or a generic blue pond dragonfly.
- Crayfish-burrow cue belongs only as a small icon inside observation card 3. Do not add a second hero animal, underground cutaway, lifecycle panel, or predation scene.
- Text layout: title and scientific name at top; exactly three numbered observation cards with small icons and explanatory two-line copy; quiet status footer at bottom.
