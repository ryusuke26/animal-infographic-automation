### Candidate 1 — Asprete / アスプレテ / *Romanichthys valsanicola* — selected

- Global familiarity check: English exposure is concentrated in Romanian conservation, European freshwater-fish, and specialist biodiversity material; Japanese exposure is sparse and does not resemble a recurring zoo, aquarium, wildlife-video, or generic weird-animal staple. The familiar small-fish silhouette does not make the species itself internationally familiar.
- Discovery doorway: this small mottled perch relative hides under stones in cold, fast water by day and emerges at night to hunt current-loving mayfly and stonefly larvae.
- Conservation doorway: current IUCN assessment `T19740A137285480` is Global Critically Endangered (CR) under B1ab(iii), assessed 1 September 2023 and published in the 2024-2 release.
- Local-knowledge caution: `asprete`, `poprete`, `popete`, and `sforete` are local Romanian names, and Romanian scientists and Vâlsan communities have long known the fish. Public copy presents a viewer's encounter in a named Romanian river, never claims universal ignorance.
- Viability: FishBase morphology and photographs plus a museum specimen record lock the elongated mottled body, broad pectoral fins, separated dorsal fins, inferior horseshoe-shaped mouth, and rocky stream setting. The under-stone-to-night-hunt progression is more specific than a generic fish profile.

### Candidate 3 — *Nymphaea thermarum*

- Global familiarity check: the species is unfamiliar outside botany and aquatic-plant circles, but its “world's smallest waterlily” identity recurs in Kew, Japanese WWF material, horticultural media, and record-book coverage. That repeated superlative exposure weakens the global-unfamiliarity gate.
- Discovery doorway: centimetre-scale pads and small white flowers grow in warm, wet mud rather than the deeper water expected of a waterlily.
- Conservation doorway: the directly inspected current official IUCN record `T185459A249717119` is Global Critically Endangered (CR) under B2ab(iii,v), assessed 25 March 2024.
- Local-knowledge caution: future wording would locate the species in Rwanda and avoid presenting international cultivation as the beginning of local knowledge.
- Ranking: direct evidence, naming, and visual-reference gates pass, but repeated “smallest waterlily” coverage makes it a weaker international-discovery choice than Asprete.

## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted scientific name: *Romanichthys valsanicola* Dumitrescu, Bănărescu & Stoica, 1957, supported by the Natural History Museum of Denmark collection record and FishBase.
- English public name: Asprete, supported by FishBase, EUNIS, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Recorded English aliases are Romanian Darter, Sculpin-perch, and Vilsan Perch.
- Romanian local names and identity aliases: asprete, poprete, popete, and sforete.
- Japanese public display name: アスプレテ. No established standard Japanese common name was confirmed in Japanese institutional or general-audience results; this is a transparent katakana rendering of the supported name. The caveat stays here and in source/context copy, not on the poster.
- Editorial classification group: Fishes.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Actinopterygii; Perciformes; Percidae; Luciopercinae; *Romanichthys*.
- Broad native region: Vâlsan River in the Argeș basin, Romania / Europe.

## Exactly three public claims

1. Habitat: it lives close to the rocky bottom of cold, fast, clear water in Romania's Vâlsan River.
2. Visible identity: it is a small elongated brown-grey fish with irregular dark mottling that breaks up its outline against the riverbed.
3. Behaviour and life history: it hides beneath stones by day and emerges at night to hunt current-loving mayfly and stonefly larvae.

## Visual identity lock

- One dominant complete adult in a stable natural side three-quarter view, hovering or resting just above a Vâlsan River stone.
- Small elongated body, large blunt slightly flattened head, rounded snout, large eyes high on the head, inferior horseshoe-shaped mouth, broad fan-like pectoral fins, small pelvic fins, and two clearly separated dorsal fins: a short spiny first dorsal and a longer soft-rayed second dorsal.
- Brown-grey to muted yellow-brown body with irregular dark brown mottling; paler underside; translucent brown-speckled fins. Camouflage must break the outline against stones without turning into stripes, bright spots, or aquarium colours.
- The near pectoral and pelvic fins must each trace continuously from the body to separate complete endpoints. Keep the far fins visibly distinct or naturally occluded, never duplicated or floating.
- Habitat is a shallow cold mountain river with rounded grey-brown stones, clear fast current, restrained algae, and broken underwater light—not an aquarium, deep lake, coral reef, muddy pond, or decorative stream.
- Nearest false silhouettes to avoid: goby, bullhead or sculpin, loach, gudgeon, zander juvenile, trout fry, aquarium cichlid, generic perch, or brightly coloured darter.
- Do not use the unsupported “living fossil from the dinosaur age” claim in public copy or as visual text.

## Discovery doorway

A small mottled fish seems to vanish beneath Vâlsan River stones by day, then comes out after dark to hunt insect larvae along the current-washed bottom.
