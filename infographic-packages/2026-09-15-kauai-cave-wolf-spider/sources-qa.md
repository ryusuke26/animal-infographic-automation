## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted scientific name: *Adelocosa anops* Gertsch, 1973, accepted by the World Spider Catalog and ITIS; it is the type and only accepted species of *Adelocosa*.
- English public name: Kauaʻi Cave Wolf Spider. The USFWS profile also uses Kauai cave wolf or peʻe peʻe makaʻole spider.
- Japanese public name: カウアイ洞窟オオカミグモ, a transparent rendering for this package, not a claimed standardized Japanese arachnid-list name.
- Editorial classification group: Other invertebrates.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Arthropoda; Chelicerata; Arachnida; Araneae; Lycosidae; *Adelocosa*.
- Broad native region: Kōloa Basin, Kauaʻi, Hawaiʻi / Oceania.
- Habitat: obligate cave-dweller in lava tubes and other cave-bearing rock; humid dark-zone cave habitat is material.
- Curiosity hook: the normal wolf-spider eye pattern disappears completely, yet the animal still actively stalks prey in darkness.

## Visual identity

- Individual/stage: one lone adult hero; sex is not claimed. A parent with newly hatched spiderlings appears only as the complete secondary miniature in Card 3.
- Reference roles: `evidence/adelocosa_anops_usfws_spiderlings_2005.webp` controls the eye-free face, low two-part wolf-spider body, reddish-brown carapace, pale abdomen, orange-brown legs, leg attachment and parent/spiderling relationship. The photograph is an identity reference only, never an edit target. `evidence/iucn_adelocosa_anops_assessment_1996.pdf` and its page capture control global status, criteria, scope, assessment date, publication year and caveat. `evidence/adelocosa_anops_usfws_5year_review_2022.pdf` controls current U.S. federal status and habitat context, not anatomy.
- Diagnostic features: (1) absolutely no eyes or eye spots on the cephalothorax; (2) reddish-brown carapace contrasting with a paler unpatterned abdomen; (3) four attached pairs of orange to orange-brown walking legs, low and moderately long rather than harvestman-thin; (4) unusually long fine sensory hairs on the front-leg tibiae and all feet; (5) adult total length roughly 12.7–19.0 mm.
- Natural pose: low lateral-dorsal three-quarter walk over damp porous lava inside a dark-zone crevice. The near-side four legs are readable; parts of far-side legs may overlap naturally behind the body or rock. No visible leg may split, merge or attach to the abdomen.
- False silhouette: not a generic eyed wolf spider or tarantula (no eyes, no dark facial eye cluster, body remains small and lightly built); not an insect or ant (eight legs, no antennae, two main body sections); not a harvestman or whip spider (not one fused round body, not extremely threadlike legs, no whip-like first pair).
- Uncertainty: the USFWS reference uses flash and shows a parent carrying pale spiderlings, so its black reflective mouthparts are not eyes and the pale round forms on the abdomen are young, not body markings. Fine tooth and spine counts are not turned into illustrated claims.
