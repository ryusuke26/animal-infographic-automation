# Sources and QA

## Claim Check Summary

- Name: `マエガミジカ` is supported by Kotobank; `Tufted deer` and *Elaphodus cephalophus* are supported by Mammal Diversity Database and U.S. FWS.
- Taxonomy: Mammal Diversity Database lists Mammalia / Artiodactyla / Cervidae / Muntiacini / *Elaphodus* / *Elaphodus cephalophus*.
- Range: public copy uses broad `southern China and northern Myanmar` wording. MDD and U.S. FWS support China/Myanmar; ADW lists northeast Myanmar plus southern and central China; Kotobank gives southern China, Tibet, Myanmar.
- Habitat: ADW supports high-elevation forest and rainforest valley habitat, often near water.
- Traits: ADW and Kotobank support the forehead tuft, short antlers, long upper canines, dark brown-gray coat, and white underside of the tail.
- Behavior/diet: ADW supports solitary/pair tendency, alarm bark, crepuscular activity, and browsing/grazing on leaves, twigs, fruits, grasses.
- Status: public copy uses `IUCN Red List 2015: Near Threatened (NT)`. Han et al. 2020 cites Harris & Jiang 2015 with the IUCN identifier `e.T7112A22159620`; MDD and ADW display Near Threatened.

## Publication Risk Notes

- Do not call it `endangered`; use Near Threatened only.
- Do not include older population or kill estimates in public copy.
- Do not overdramatize the canines as vampire fangs. `犬歯 / canines` is safer than `牙 / fangs` for main factual labels.
- Avoid exact distribution maps and subspecies boundaries.
- Conservation pressures may be mentioned only as source-context: hunting/trade and habitat loss are cited, but the package does not center blame or urgency.

## Visual QA Checklist

- Small deer, not goat, antelope, or generic fawn.
- Dark forehead tuft visible.
- Tiny antlers are short and simple, not large branching antlers.
- Upper canines visible but natural.
- Mountain forest understory with moist greenery.
- No fake map, no population-number imagery, no rescue/blame symbolism.
