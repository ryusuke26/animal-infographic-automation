# Sources and QA — Numbat

## Evidence Lock

- Accepted English common name: Numbat
- Accepted scientific name: *Myrmecobius fasciatus* Waterhouse, 1836
- Japanese public title: フクロアリクイ
- Japanese naming basis: the Mammalogical Society of Japan's 2018 Japanese mammal-name list records *Myrmecobius fasciatus* as フクロアリクイ. `https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/mammalianscience/58/Supplement/58_S1/_pdf`
- Taxonomy: Mammalia; Dasyuromorphia; Myrmecobiidae. It is the sole living species of Myrmecobiidae.
- Broad native region: Australia/Oceania
- Native range: endemic to Australia. Natural remnant populations occur in south-west Western Australia, with reintroduced populations elsewhere in Australia. Public copy stays at the broad Australia level and does not imply that every population is natural.
- Habitat: eucalypt woodlands and forests with old and fallen hollow logs used for shelter and nesting; logs and woodland soils also support termite food.
- Locked behavior: unlike most marsupials, numbats are active by day; they use strong foreclaws to open shallow termite galleries and a long narrow sticky tongue to collect termites.
- Exact visual identity: small slender reddish-brown terrestrial marsupial; long pointed snout; black stripe through each eye; bold white transverse bands across the rear back and rump; long bushy tail; four ordinary walking limbs with clawed feet; upright ears.
- Lookalike guard: not a true anteater, bandicoot, quoll, mongoose, squirrel, chipmunk, skunk, thylacine, or echidna. Do not add an anteater-sized body, tubular toothless muzzle, pouch, spines, hooves, horns, forehead points, facial projections near the eyes, or black-and-white skunk coloration.
