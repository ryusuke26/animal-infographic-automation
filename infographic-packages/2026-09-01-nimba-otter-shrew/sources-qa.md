### Candidate 1 — Nimba Otter-shrew / *Micropotamogale lamottei* — selected

- Global familiarity check: English exposure remains concentrated in Afrotheria specialist reports, taxonomy databases, and small-mammal papers; Japanese exposure is mostly a standard-name list and encyclopedia references. It is not a recurring zoo, pet, global wildlife-video, viral, or generic weird-animal staple.
- Discovery doorway: a small dark Afrotherian follows forest streams and finds prey underwater with sensitive whiskers and an enlarged upper lip, although its feet are unwebbed and its tail is not a broad paddle.
- Conservation doorway: formal IUCN assessment `T13393A111940150` is Global Vulnerable (VU) under B1ab(i,ii,iii,v), assessed 20 December 2017 and published in 2018. The official 2018-2 change table records its change from NT to VU.
- Local-knowledge caution: public copy names the Nimba landscape and frames the animal as a discovery for the viewer; it does not claim that communities in Guinea, Liberia, or Cote d'Ivoire lack knowledge of the animal or its streams.
- Viability: the peer-reviewed Mount Nimba study includes a captured adult photograph and locks the stream habitat, small rounded body, uniform dark coat, short legs, long muscular near-round tail, and low-density stream-edge life. The prompt explicitly forbids a true otter, generic shrew, webbed feet, and a wide flattened tail. Mammals is absent from the latest eight, but unfamiliarity, the non-obvious aquatic mechanism, supported status, standard Japanese name, and usable visual reference are the primary reasons for selection.

### Candidate 2 — White-winged Flufftail / *Sarothrura ayresi*

- Global familiarity check: English exposure is concentrated in African wetland-conservation and specialist birding material; Japanese general-audience exposure is sparse. The broader small-rail silhouette is familiar, but this species is not a mainstream wildlife fixture.
- Discovery doorway: an elusive high-altitude wetland bird reveals bright white secondaries only when its wings open.
- Conservation doorway: formal IUCN/BirdLife assessment `T22692245A179602552` records Global Critically Endangered (CR) in the 2021 assessment; current South African material distinguishes this from the separate 2025 regional EN assessment.
- Local-knowledge caution: Ethiopian and South African wetland communities, researchers, and bird-conservation teams have longstanding knowledge of its seasonal sites.
- Ranking: evidence and unfamiliarity pass, but a stable closed-wing hero hides the white diagnostic feathers while an open-wing pose raises anatomy and composition risk. It remains below the selected mammal.

### Candidate 3 — St Helena Ebony / *Melhania ebenus* (IUCN assessment under *Trochetiopsis ebenus*)

- Global familiarity check: English exposure is concentrated in St Helena and Kew plant-conservation material; Japanese general-audience exposure is sparse and not a recurring horticultural staple.
- Discovery doorway: a white-flowered cliff shrub thought lost for roughly a century survived on an inaccessible cliff and became the source of propagated plants.
- Conservation doorway: the official IUCN route `T37855A67371855` records CR under the older accepted combination *Trochetiopsis ebenus*.
- Local-knowledge caution: any future copy must credit St Helena discovery, propagation, and living knowledge instead of framing recovery as an outside discovery.
- Ranking: not selected because Kew now accepts *Melhania ebenus*, while the current IUCN record remains under *Trochetiopsis ebenus* and its exact field-level assessment year was not directly exposed in this screen. That naming/date reconciliation is unnecessary for today's stronger candidate.

## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted scientific name: *Micropotamogale lamottei* Heim de Balsac, 1954.
- English public name: Nimba Otter-shrew, used by the Mammal Diversity Database, ITIS, GBIF, and the IUCN assessment.
- English alias: Pygmy Otter-shrew.
- Japanese public name: ヒメポタモガーレ, listed for *Micropotamogale lamottei* in the Mammal Society of Japan's 2018 standard world-mammal name list.
- Editorial classification group: Mammals.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Chordata; Mammalia; Afrotheria; Afrosoricida; Potamogalidae; *Micropotamogale*.
- Broad native region: Mount Nimba and nearby hills across Guinea, Liberia, and Cote d'Ivoire, plus the Putu Range in Liberia / West Africa / Africa.
- Habitat: small rivers and mid-elevation forest streams, including fragmented forest and densely vegetated stream margins.
- Curiosity hook: an aquatic hunter with sensitive whiskers but without webbed feet or a broad paddle tail.

## Visual identity lock

- Reference role: the adult photograph in Monadjem et al. is a species-identity reference only, never an edit target.
- Hero: exactly one complete small adult at the edge of a shaded West African forest stream, body low over a wet stone with the snout and whiskers angled toward shallow water. Natural far-limb occlusion is allowed.
- Body: compact rounded body; dense sleek uniform dark chocolate to grey-brown fur; short neck; elongated whiskered muzzle with a soft broad upper-lip pad; tiny dark eyes; small rounded ears; four short unwebbed limbs; five small toes per visible foot; long muscular slender tail, round or only very slightly compressed, tapering naturally.
- Scale and posture: rat-sized rather than otter-sized; low stable quadrupedal posture supported by the stone, not swimming in open water and not standing upright.
- Habitat: cool shaded mid-elevation forest stream with clear shallow water, wet dark stones, submerged leaf litter, small current lines, moss, roots, and restrained broadleaf foliage. No ocean, lake, rice-field panorama, aquarium, or fantasy jungle.
- Card art: card 1 shows a forest-stream cross-section; card 2 shows one complete small side-view animal with the round body and slender tail; card 3 shows one complete small animal lowering its whiskered muzzle between shallow stones toward a crab or aquatic invertebrate.
- False silhouettes to avoid: true otter, mink, muskrat, water vole, Eurasian water shrew, elephant shrew/sengi, hedgehog-like tenrec, ordinary mouse, generic pointed shrew, beaver, platypus, webbed feet, flipper feet, broad laterally flattened paddle tail, long visible external ears, white belly, stripes, spots, spines, or oversized eyes.
