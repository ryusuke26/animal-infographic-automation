# Sources and QA

## Claim Check

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence | Public handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Spirobranchus giganteus* is accepted and marine. | accurate | WoRMS lists status as accepted, species rank, marine environment, and original name *Serpula gigantea* Pallas, 1766. | Use full scientific name and authority in README. |
| Classification is Annelida / Polychaeta / Sabellida / Serpulidae. | accurate | GBIF Backbone Taxonomy lists Animalia, Annelida, Polychaeta, Sabellida, Serpulidae, *Spirobranchus*. | Use "多毛類・カンザシゴカイ科" and "family Serpulidae". |
| English common name is Christmas tree worm and Japanese common name is イバラカンザシ. | accurate with context | GBIF and WoRMS list those vernacular names. | Japanese poster foregrounds イバラカンザシ. |
| The visible spiral crowns are radioles used for respiration and feeding. | accurate | NOAA National Ocean Service describes the crowns as radioles used for respiration and catching phytoplankton. | Use "らせんの鰓で食事と呼吸". |
| It lives with most of the body in coral/tube and retracts when startled. | accurate | NOAA says most of the body is anchored in coral burrows and the worm rapidly retracts when startled. | Use "近づくとすっと管へ隠れる". |
| Circumtropical/global range wording is straightforward. | needs context | NOAA public article uses tropical reefs around the world, while WoRMS warns that circumtropical *S. giganteus* identifications are confused and the binomen should be restricted to Caribbean specimens. | Avoid a map and use conservative Caribbean/western Atlantic wording in captions. |
| IUCN Red List status can be given as a category. | cannot verify | No direct species assessment page was located by targeted IUCN search in this run. IUCN FAQ says Not Evaluated species are not included on the Red List. | Use "assessment not located / 掲載評価未確認" rather than inventing NE or LC. |

## Image Identity Guidance

- Show two spiral, feather-like radiolar crowns emerging from a coral head.
- Show most of the worm hidden; do not show a free-swimming worm.
- Include a small operculum/lid only as a subtle anatomy cue.
- Avoid making it look like a plant, coral polyp, jellyfish, nudibranch, or decorative Christmas ornament.
- Avoid fake maps and unsupported global-distribution graphics.

## Image QA

- Japanese Image Gen: title イバラカンザシ and scientific name are readable. Visual identity shows paired red/white spiral radioles emerging from coral, with retraction and coral-tube notes. Exact generated wording has minor typography risk, so package copy and text-safe backups are authoritative.
- English Image Gen: title and scientific name are readable. Visual identity shows coral habitat, spiral gills, tube cross-section, and retraction behavior. Exact generated wording is good, with package copy as authoritative.
- Text-safe SVG and PNG backups created for both languages to preserve exact wording.
