- Global familiarity check: English exposure is concentrated in Australian government, biodiversity, and specialist conservation material; Japanese general-audience searches did not surface a recurring zoo, aquarium, wildlife-video, or generic weird-animal staple.
- Discovery doorway: a fish only about 3 cm long lives in sunlit desert artesian springs less than 8 cm deep, and breeding males show bright red fins.
- Conservation doorway: current IUCN route is Global Critically Endangered (CR), assessed 11 February 2019, assessment `T19951A123379010`.
- Local-knowledge caution: copy says this is a viewer discovery, never that the fish is unknown in Queensland; Australian and Traditional Owner stewardship is not erased.

## Selected identity and naming

## Exactly three public claims

1. Habitat: it occupies sparsely vegetated desert-spring wetlands less than 8 cm deep at Edgbaston Springs.
2. Visible identity: it is a slender freshwater fish reaching only about 3 cm, with a conspicuous blue eye.
3. Life history: breeding males display bright red fins.

## Visual identity lock

- One dominant mature male, shown in a stable side profile swimming through extremely shallow clear spring water.
- Slender laterally compressed olive-gold and silvery translucent body, small blunt head, conspicuous turquoise-blue ring around the dark eye, narrow rounded tail.
- Scarlet-orange is localized on the small dorsal, pelvic, anal, and outer tail fins; do not paint the whole fish red.
- Habitat must read as a sunlit Great Artesian Basin spring among sedges, pale mineral sand, and dry ochre country—not an aquarium, reef, deep river, or blue open-water scene.
- Nearest false silhouettes to avoid: fancy guppy, neon tetra, medaka, generalized deep-bodied rainbowfish, trout fry, salmon fry, coral-reef fish.
- The DCCEEW photograph was directly inspected in the browser on 2026-08-20; ALA also exposes live and type-reference images.

## Discovery lock

Lead with the spatial contradiction: desert ground, water shallower than a hand is wide, and a fish living there. Let the story move from shallow spring, to 3 cm scale, to the red breeding fins. Conservation remains a quiet footer, not the emotional opening.
