## Selected identity and naming

- Accepted scientific name: *Achatinella mustelina* Mighels, 1845. The current IUCN record uses this species-level name. Holland and Hadfield (2007) synonymized the named shell-based subspecies with the species while recognizing six evolutionarily significant units for conservation management.
- English public name: Oʻahu Tree Snail. USFWS uses Oahu tree snails for genus *Achatinella*; NatureServe records Oahu Treesnail for *A. mustelina*. The ʻokina is retained in public display copy.
- Japanese public name: オアフ・ツリースネイル, a transparent rendering of the supported English name. No established Japanese zoological standard name was confirmed; this caveat stays in evidence notes rather than poster copy.
- Hawaiian-name context: current and historical Hawaiʻi sources use kāhuli and forms including Pupu Kani Oe for Hawaiian tree snails. These names are not substituted into species-level public copy because source scope and orthography vary.
- Editorial classification group: Other invertebrates.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Mollusca; Gastropoda; Stylommatophora; Achatinellidae; *Achatinella*.
- Broad native region: Waiʻanae Range, Oʻahu, Hawaiian Islands, United States / central Pacific / Oceania.
- Habitat: fragmented upper-elevation mesic and wet native-forest patches, now mostly above 2,000 ft (about 610 m), where individuals live on leaves, trunks, and branches.
- Curiosity hook: a night-active land snail spends its life in trees, grazes microscopic fungi from leaves, and gives birth to one relatively large live young at a time.

## Visual identity lock

- Reference role: the three Hawaiʻi DLNR photographs are species-identity references only, never edit targets. They show real pattern variation: glossy dark brown-black shells with cream bands or zones, sometimes with a mostly pale upper spire, on living leaves or twigs.
- Hero: exactly one dominant adult on the upper surface of a broad living leaf at night.
