# Sources QA — Maned Rat / タテガミネズミ

## Verdict

Overall: Ready. Official global assessment, naming caveat, three claims, and visual identity are locked.

## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Lophiomys imhausii* A. Milne-Edwards, 1867.
- Historical IUCN citation name: *Lophiomys imhausi* Milne-Edwards, 1867.
- English common name: Maned Rat. “Crested Rat” and “African Crested Rat” are also used.
- Supported Japanese name: タテガミネズミ.
- Native region and habitat: eastern Africa; wooded and riparian habitats, with tree cavities and rocky outcrops used as potential den sites in the central Kenya study.
- Lineage: the only living species in genus *Lophiomys* and subfamily Lophiomyinae.
- IUCN check: the official species-page screenshot and matching seven-page assessment PDF directly confirm Global Least Concern (LC), assessed 31 January 2016 and published 2016, under *Lophiomys imhausi*.
- Official record: `e.T12308A22368581`; DOI `10.2305/IUCN.UK.2016-2.RLTS.T12308A22368581.en`.
- Publication-safe status wording: `IUCN Red List 2016 : 低懸念 (LC)` / `IUCN Red List 2016: Least Concern (LC)`.

## Naming caveat

The current Mammal Diversity Database accepts *Lophiomys imhausii*, matching the original spelling, and treats *Lophiomys imhausi* as an incorrect subsequent spelling. The 2018 Mammal Society of Japan list and the official IUCN assessment use *imhausi*. Public identity lines use the current accepted MDD spelling; source replies identify the spelling under which the official IUCN record is registered.

## Visual identity guidance

- One large, robust, rabbit-sized rodent in a stable side-on or three-quarter ground walk.
- Small dark eye, short rounded ear partly hidden by fur, dark muzzle and legs.
- Dense long gray-brown guard hairs and a crest from crown along the back.
- Broad black-and-white warning band along each flank, normally partly hidden beneath long fur.
- Thick, fully furred bushy tail connected at the rump; never a thin naked rat tail.
- Four short limbs, each with a visible shoulder or hip origin, separate path, paw, and ground contact.
- Not a porcupine, skunk, civet, generic laboratory rat, or striped squirrel; no quills, no duplicated hero, no detached flank patch.

## Discovery doorway

A quiet sequence: bark is chewed, the mouth moves toward the flank, and the mane parts to reveal a warning band carrying plant-derived toxin. The animal does not manufacture the toxin itself.
