## Candidate screen

### Tokashiki Freshwater Crab / トカシキオオサワガニ / *Geothelphusa levicervix* — selected

- Global familiarity check: Japanese results are mostly Okinawan red-data, museum, and taxonomic material; English results are dominated by assessment/checklist sources. It is not a household, zoo, aquarium, wildlife-video, or generic “weird animal” staple internationally.
- Discovery doorway: a broad landlocked freshwater crab walks a dim forest stream on a small Ryukyu island; a large male carries one outsized claw, while its young develop from a few large eggs rather than drifting at sea.
- Conservation doorway: the official IUCN assessment is Endangered (EN) under B1ab(iii)+2ab(iii), assessed and published in 2008 as `T134902A4033497`. The supplied current-page capture identifies this as the most recent assessment.
- Local-knowledge caution: the crab has a documented Japanese common name and has long been recorded in Okinawan natural-history work. Public wording frames a specific forest-stream encounter for a wider audience; it never says the crab is unknown on Tokashiki.

Tokashiki Freshwater Crab ranks first after the hard naming and evidence gates. It is internationally overlooked, but its Japanese name, exact scientific name, global IUCN category/year, island range, and three visual claims are all supportable. ## Evidence Lock

- Accepted scientific name: *Geothelphusa levicervix* (Rathbun, 1898).
- English common name: Tokashiki Freshwater Crab. “Tokashiki big freshwater crab” also appears in English biodiversity material; public copy uses the shorter supported rendering.
- Japanese common name: トカシキオオサワガニ.
- Editorial classification group: Other invertebrates.
- Exact lineage: Animalia; Arthropoda; Crustacea; Malacostraca; Decapoda; Brachyura; Potamidae; *Geothelphusa*; *G. levicervix*.
- Broad native region: Tokashiki Island, Ryukyu Islands, Japan / Asia.
- Habitat: moisture-retaining evergreen forest, especially shaded narrow slow-flowing streams and wet ground.
- Naming caveat: historical regional populations once treated as one “big freshwater crab” were separated by detailed research; *G. levicervix* is the Tokashiki species. No synonym is used in public copy.
- Global IUCN category: Endangered (EN), global assessment `T134902A4033497`, assessed and published 2008 under B1ab(iii)+2ab(iii). The taxon-page route is `https://www.iucnredlist.org/species/134902/4033497`.
- IUCN check: 2026-08-17. The user-supplied official IUCN PDF and matching current-page capture directly confirm Global EN, scope Global, assessment date 1 January 2008, publication year 2008, and `T134902A4033497`. They are preserved in `evidence/`. No local category is substituted for the global footer.
- Japanese status context: national and Okinawa categories are different jurisdictional assessments. They remain out of the poster, main post, and story reply.

## Visual identity guidance

- One complete large adult male is the hero: a broad, smooth, thick, strongly domed freshwater-crab carapace up to 63 mm wide, two eyestalks, two claws, and four fully separate pairs of walking legs.
- The enlarged claw belongs to one side of a large male and remains a normal crab claw. Do not show a fiddler-crab display, lobster claw, boxing-glove claw, detached claw, or more than two claws.
- The setting is a dark, moisture-retaining evergreen forest: wet stone, moss, leaf litter, a narrow slow stream, and humid ground. It is not a beach, reef, surf zone, mangrove, rice paddy, aquarium, or fast open river.
- Card 1: a complete narrow shaded forest-stream vignette. Card 2: an intact large male with unequal claws. Card 3: an intact female carrying several large eggs beneath the abdomen plus one tiny complete juvenile, so direct development is not turned into free-swimming larvae.
- Keep exactly one dominant hero and exactly three numbered illustrated cards. Do not invent a color diagnostic, add a map, show a colony, or imply a population estimate.

## Discovery doorway

On a forested Ryukyu island, a broad freshwater crab stays landlocked beside a narrow shaded stream. One large male bears an oversized claw, while the next generation begins as a few large eggs carried into tiny crabs rather than a sea-going larval drift.

## Publication cautions

- Keep the global IUCN EN footer separate from Japanese national and Okinawa Prefecture categories.
- Use the confirmed 2008 assessment year and `T134902A4033497`; do not use the PDF copyright year or a Japanese document's 2015-list context as an IUCN assessment date.
- Do not publish a population number, collection/trade claim, or a primary threat ranking; those are not needed for this discovery-first package.
- Do not say the crab is unknown to Tokashiki or Okinawan communities.
- Do not turn direct development into a marine larval-stage claim. The supported wording is only that a few large eggs develop directly into small crabs.
- Do not confuse the species with other Ryukyu freshwater crabs, marine crabs, fiddler crabs, hermit crabs, lobsters, or crayfish.
