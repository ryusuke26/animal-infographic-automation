## Evidence Notes

- Birds of the World lists the species as "Sword-billed Hummingbird *Ensifera ensifera*" and gives Japanese as `ヤリハシハチドリ`.
- Birds of the World identifies *Ensifera ensifera* as monotypic and gives the protonym *Ornismya ensifera* Boissonneau, 1840. No subspecies split is used in public copy.
- The native range is the Andes from western Venezuela through Colombia, Ecuador and Peru to north-central Bolivia.
- eBird describes the species as found in the temperate zone of the Andes from Venezuela to Bolivia and in montane forest and edges.
- Birds of the World says the bill is slightly upcurved and longer than body length, and that this is the only bird species with bill length exceeding body length.
- Birds of the World and eBird support feeding at long tubular flowers. Birds of the World lists long pendent corollas including *Passiflora mixta* and notes trap-line foraging.
- The user-provided official IUCN screenshot shows Sword-billed Hummingbird / *Ensifera ensifera*, Least Concern (LC), Global scope, and Last assessed 12 June 2024. Public copy uses a quiet IUCN status footer and avoids threat, population-size, and trend claims.

## Visual Identity Guidance

- Show one small hummingbird, not a toucan, sunbird, kingfisher, bee, or generic short-billed hummingbird.
- Core visual marks: extremely long straight-to-slightly-upturned black bill, green body, bronzy/coppery head, small white spot behind the eye, narrow wings, forked dark tail.
- Natural posture: perched near a long tubular pendant flower or hovering calmly near flowers; if perched, bill held diagonally upward.
- Habitat cues: misty Andean cloud forest edge, mossy branches, tubular flowers, soft mountain greenery. No map.
- Avoid: metal sword beak, oversized weapon symbolism, human hands, feeders as the main scene, tropical lowland jungle look, multiple species comparison panels, fake range map, threat/drama imagery.
