## Compact visual identity brief
Stage: brown adult female hero using full-body museum reference, green adult contextual chest study allowed as documented within-species color variation. Female broader than male; no fixed size/segment/spine count. No juvenile art necessary.
Reference roles (biological identity only): potsdam-adult-female.jpg (M. Altgott) exact female hanging posture, long prothorax, closed wings and connected six-leg plan; potsdam-portrait.jpg (M. Altgott) triangular head, large compound eyes/two threadlike antennae, inner coxal black marks and folding spined foreleg; potsdam-ootheca.jpg (M. Keller) attachment, raised tan ridges and narrow central seam. Three distinct references, no gallery export or fourth photo. Existing purple-frog benchmark is style-only.
Defining features:
1. Portrait head: broad uncrested triangular head with two large lateral compound eyes and two fine antennae; small mouth at triangle tip, no extra eyes/helmet.
2. Female full-body: elongated slender prothorax continuously joins small head and wing-covered broader abdomen. Closed long forewings lie parallel over abdomen, not leaf lobes or a dorsal crest.
3. Female front legs: paired forelegs originate at anterior thorax; elongated coxae join thick spined femora and folded spined tibiae with fine terminal feet. Four thinner middle/hind walking legs arise from posterior thorax. Natural far-leg occlusion allowed; six-leg plan is not six full visible limbs.
4. Portrait inner coxa: small black oval at basal inner surface, pale center varies; not a black wing patch or mandatory matching bilateral white dots from this angle.
5. Egg reference: elongated convex tan foam case securely attached along stem, layered transverse texture and narrow lengthwise seam. Not round exposed eggs, a silken spider sac, fruit, hive or floating capsule.
Natural pose: supported hanging/lateral three-quarter female on an attached herb stem, abdomen to left and head toward right; legs may overlap vegetation or body. Never spread hidden legs merely to count them. All visible limbs must trace to thorax. Chest study retains head/prothorax/foreleg connection. No threat display exposing unknown hidden anatomy, no internal feeding structures.
False silhouettes: avoid Empusa head crest/leg lobes, grasshopper enlarged jumping hind femora, generic Tenodera with no diagnostic coxal context.
Uncertainty: spine numbers, egg numbers, exact scale and markings not quantitative. Pale coxal center may hide; green/brown color not a sex classifier.
