## Taxonomy And Naming Caveat

The accepted name remains *Rhizanthella gardneri*. Older sources treated
southern Western Australian populations near Munglinup as this species.
Dixon and Christenhusz described those southern plants as
*Rhizanthella johnstonii* in 2018. Public range wording therefore follows the
current Kew and FloraBase scope and does not repeat the older two-area range.

## Locked Public Claims

1. *Rhizanthella gardneri* is native to south-western Western Australia and is
   associated with sandy soil and litter beneath broom honey-myrtle thickets.
2. It is a leafless subterranean orchid whose flower head develops under the
   soil and may reach the surface as it matures.
3. It lacks photosynthetic leaves and obtains carbon and nutrients through a
   mycorrhizal fungus connected with *Melaleuca*.

## Visual Identity Guidance

Show one coherent *Rhizanthella gardneri* flower head at the soil surface
beneath loose brown leaf and bark litter in a Western Australian broom
honey-myrtle thicket. The head should be small, low, rounded, and
tulip-like: six to twelve fleshy cream to pale pink outer bracts curve inward
around many tiny inward-facing cream, pink, and deep reddish flowers. A shallow
educational soil cutaway may show one pale horizontal succulent rhizome below
the head, fine fungal threads, and nearby *Melaleuca* roots. Keep the cutaway
simple and secondary to the single flower head.

Do not show green leaves, an above-ground leafy stem, a tall orchid spike,
large showy orchid petals, a single tulip, a mushroom, a rafflesia-like flower,
multiple specimens, exposed roots arranged like an anatomy chart, fake maps,
people, population counters, rescue imagery, or unsupported pollinator action.
